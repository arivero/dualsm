\title{\textbf{Mass Exponents and Supercurrent Tests\\
at the Conformal Window}}
\author{Notes from the Dualised Standard Model Programme}
\date{March 2026, revised November 2026}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Antipin, Mojaza, Pica, and Sannino found a supersymmetric solution
of the perturbative gauge and Yukawa fixed-point equations in a
candidate magnetic dual of QCD with an adjoint Weyl fermion.  We ask
which consequences can be tested with only fundamental fermions.
In $\mathcal N=1$ SQCD the quark mass sources the $F$-component of
the chiral meson $Q\widetilde Q$, giving $\gamma_m=(3\Nc-\Nf)/\Nf$.
For $\SU{3}$ with eight flavours the result is $1/8$. In fundamental-only QCD it is a benchmark with no known matching mechanism.  Transferring
it to fundamental-only QCD requires an infrared fixed point whose
leading quark mass operator has dimension $23/8$.  This assumption
predicts a Dirac mode number per unit volume proportional to
$\Lambda_R^{32/9}$.  A vector flavour symmetry obstructs a local
$\mathcal N=1$ supercurrent in the eight-flavour target theory.
More generally, baryon number and unbroken charge conjugation exclude
a single local Weyl supercharge in fundamental-only vectorlike QCD.
Adding an adjoint Weyl fermion changes the operator algebra. A
six-flavour example passes the anomaly and symmetry tests below and
admits a lattice Ward identity with a translation contact term.
Ten and twelve flavours lie outside the interacting SQCD window and
provide non-supersymmetric controls.
The November derivations received internal model review; independent
peer review and priority for the supercurrent exclusion remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The conformal window of a non-abelian gauge theory is the range of
matter content for which the theory possesses an infrared-stable fixed
point: the theory is asymptotically free in the UV but flows to a
conformal field theory in the IR.  For $\SU{\Nc}$ with $\Nf$ Dirac
fundamental flavours, asymptotic freedom is lost at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:AF-bound}
  \Nf^{\AF} = \frac{11\Nc}{2}\,,
\end{equation}
giving $\Nf^{\AF} = 16.5$ for $\SU{3}$.  Below $\Nf^{\AF}$, asymptotic
freedom holds.  As $\Nf$ is decreased, the theory undergoes a transition
at some critical value $\Nfstar$: for $\Nf < \Nfstar$ the theory
confines and breaks chiral symmetry spontaneously, while for
$\Nfstar < \Nf < \Nf^{\AF}$ the theory flows to an infrared fixed
point.  Near the upper edge this fixed point is perturbatively
accessible~\cite{BZ1982,Caswell1974}.  The location of the lower edge
for $\SU{3}$ remains a lattice question.  We consider the range
$8\lesssim\Nfstar\lesssim12$ explored in
Refs.~\cite{Hasenbusch2017,Appelquist2016,Aoki2014,Fodor2012}.

Antipin, Mojaza, Pica, and Sannino (AMPS)~\cite{AMPS2013} studied a
candidate magnetic dual of $\SU{\Nc}$ with $\Nf$ Dirac flavours and
one adjoint Weyl fermion.  For the magnetic $\SU{X}$ theory with
$X=\Nf-\Nc$, their perturbative gauge and Yukawa beta functions admit
a fixed point with Seiberg's supersymmetric coupling relations and a
basin of attraction in the couplings studied.  Their expansion is
controlled just below $\Nf=3X$, the loss of magnetic asymptotic
freedom.  In electric variables this is just above
$\Nf=\tfrac32\Nc$.  The eight-flavour case at $\Nc=3$ is far from
that lower SQCD edge.

The lattice target has gauge group $\SU{3}$ and $\Nf$ Dirac
fundamentals.  Removing the adjoint Weyl fermion changes the electric
theory and its global symmetries.  The AMPS calculation supplies no
operator map for this change of field content.  We ask whether the
eight-flavour theory has an IR fixed point at the proposed boundary
and whether its fermion mass has the SQCD scaling exponent.
\Cref{ssec:mass-map} states the extra assumption and checks whether a
literal SQCD multiplet can realise it.  The ten- and twelve-flavour
theories serve as controls outside the SQCD window.

\section{The non-SUSY conformal window and lattice status}
\label{sec:review}

\subsection{Perturbative constraints on the conformal window}

The one-loop coefficient of the beta function for $\SU{\Nc}$ with
$\Nf$ Dirac fundamental flavours is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:b0}
  b_0 = \frac{11\Nc - 2\Nf}{3}\,.
\end{equation}
Asymptotic freedom ($b_0 > 0$) requires $\Nf < 11\Nc/2$.  The
two-loop coefficient is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:b1}
  b_1 = \frac{1}{3}\left(
    34\Nc^2 - \Nf\left(10\Nc + \frac{3(\Nc^2 - 1)}{\Nc}\right)
  \right).
\end{equation}
With the two-loop beta function in the standard normalisation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-2loop}
  \mu\frac{d\alpha}{d\mu} = -\frac{b_0}{2\pi}\,\alpha^2
  - \frac{b_1}{8\pi^2}\,\alpha^3\,,
\end{equation}
the two-loop IR zero occurs at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-BZ}
  \alpha_* = -\frac{4\pi\, b_0}{b_1}
\end{equation}
when $b_0>0$ and $b_1<0$.  For $\SU{3}$ this gives
$153/19<\Nf<33/2$, or $9\leq\Nf\leq16$ for integer
$\Nf$~\cite{BZ1982,Caswell1974}.  Near the upper edge perturbation
theory is controlled.  A strong-coupling zero of the truncated beta
function cannot locate the lower edge reliably.

\subsection{Non-perturbative estimates}

Several approaches give estimates for $\Nfstar$:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{Schwinger--Dyson (ladder approximation):}
    Chiral symmetry breaking occurs when the mass anomalous dimension
    $\gamma_m$ exceeds unity.  Combined with the two-loop running, this
    gives $\Nfstar \approx 4\Nc = 12$ for $\SU{3}$~\cite{ATW1996}.

  \item \textbf{All-orders conjectured beta function
    (Ryttov--Sannino):}  Using a conjectured non-perturbative beta
    function inspired by the NSVZ form, Ryttov and
    Sannino~\cite{RS2008} estimated $\Nfstar \approx (11/4)\Nc = 8.25$
    for $\SU{3}$, with the lower boundary corresponding to
    $\gamma_m = 2$.

  \item \textbf{Functional RG and other methods:}  Various functional
    RG and truncated Schwinger--Dyson studies give
    $\Nfstar \in [8, 12]$~\cite{Gies2006,Braun2010}.
\end{enumerate}

The spread of estimates underscores the need for non-perturbative
lattice calculations.

\subsection{Lattice status for SU(3)}
\label{ssec:lattice-status}

Several collaborations have performed large-scale lattice simulations
of $\SU{3}$ with varying numbers of fundamental flavours:

\textbf{$\boldsymbol{\Nf = 8}$:}  The LatKMI
collaboration~\cite{LatKMI2014_Nf8} and the LSD
collaboration~\cite{LSD2014_Nf8} have studied this theory extensively.
The spectrum shows a light scalar ($\sigma$) state with mass comparable
to the pseudoscalar ($\pi$), reminiscent of a dilaton or
pseudo-Nambu--Goldstone boson of approximate scale invariance.
LatKMI hyperscaling fits give effective mass exponents
$\gamma_m\simeq 1$ in several channels and $\simeq 0.6$ for $m_\pi$;
the Dirac spectrum also shows a coupling dependence~\cite{LatKMI2017_Nf8,Cheng2013}.
These results admit slow running near the conformal boundary.  They
leave the existence of an eight-flavour IR fixed point open.

\textbf{$\boldsymbol{\Nf = 10}$.}  Running-coupling studies include
Schr\"odinger-functional and gradient-flow step
scaling~\cite{Hayakawa2011,HRW2020}.  This flavour number is a useful
control for the location of the lower edge of the QCD window.

\textbf{$\boldsymbol{\Nf = 12}$.}  The LatKMI
collaboration~\cite{LatKMI2012_Nf12} found finite-size scaling
consistent with conformality and $\gamma_m\simeq0.4$--$0.5$.
Fodor \textit{et al.}~\cite{Fodor2012} argued for chiral symmetry
breaking in their data.  These studies illustrate the difficulty of
establishing the infrared limit.

\section{SUSY predictions for the conformal window boundary}
\label{sec:susy-predictions}

The protected dimensions below refer to massless SQCD at the origin of
its moduli space.  Their application to a non-supersymmetric theory
requires a separate statement about that theory's operators.

\subsection{The NSVZ beta function and the SUSY conformal window}

In $\mathcal{N}=1$ SQCD with gauge group $\SU{\Nc}$ and $\Nf$
fundamental chiral flavour pairs $(Q_i, \widetilde{Q}_i)$, the exact
NSVZ beta function~\cite{NSVZ1983} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:NSVZ}
  \beta(\alpha) = -\frac{\alpha^2}{2\pi}\,
  \frac{3\Nc - \Nf(1 - \gamma_Q(\alpha))}
       {1 - \Nc\,\alpha/(2\pi)}\,,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_Q$ is the anomalous dimension of the fundamental chiral
superfield~$Q$.  At an interacting zero in a regular coupling
coordinate the numerator vanishes, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gammaQ-star}
  \gamma_Q^* = 1 - \frac{3\Nc}{\Nf}\,.
\end{equation}
The SUSY conformal window is the range where the fixed point exists
and the gauge-invariant chiral primaries respect the unitarity bound
$\dim(\mathcal{O}) \geq 1$.  The bound applies to gauge-invariant
operators, so it constrains the meson $M = Q\widetilde{Q}$ rather than
the quark superfield: with $\dim(Q) = 1 + \gamma_Q/2$ (free-field
dimension~$1$), $\dim(M) = 2 + \gamma_Q^* \geq 1$ requires
$\gamma_Q^* \geq -1$, \ie\ $\Nf \geq \tfrac{3}{2}\Nc$.  Asymptotic
freedom, $3\Nc - \Nf > 0$, supplies the upper edge.  The resulting
window is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SUSY-window}
  \frac{3}{2}\Nc < \Nf < 3\Nc\,.
\end{equation}
For $\SU{3}$: $4.5 < \Nf < 9$.

\subsection{The mass anomalous dimension}
\label{ssec:gamma-m}

The key quantity for lattice comparison is the mass anomalous dimension
$\gamma_m$, defined by the scaling dimension of the fermion bilinear:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-m-def}
  \dim(\bar\psi\psi) = 3 - \gamma_m\,.
\end{equation}
Which component occurs in this definition can be read off from the
source.  In electric SQCD the mass is a constant superpotential coupling
to the chiral meson $M^i{}_j=Q^i\widetilde Q_j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass-source}
  \delta{\cal L}=\int d^2\theta\,m^j{}_i M^i{}_j+\mathrm{h.c.}
  =m^j{}_i(F_M)^i{}_j+\mathrm{h.c.}
\end{equation}
The lowest component $\phi_M=q\tilde q$ is a squark bilinear.
Multiplication of the two chiral superfields gives
$F_M=F_Q\tilde q+qF_{\widetilde Q}-\psi_Q\psi_{\widetilde Q}$, with
flavour indices suppressed.  At zero mass and with no tree
superpotential the constituent auxiliary fields vanish on shell.
Thus a real common mass probes the scalar Dirac density in
$\Tr F_M+\mathrm{h.c.}$, up to the mass sign convention.  At nonzero
mass eliminating the auxiliaries also generates scalar mass terms of
order $|m|^2$.  The linear insertion at $m=0$ is $F_M$.

At a superconformal fixed point the dimension of a chiral primary
operator~$\mathcal{O}$ with R-charge $R(\mathcal{O})$ is fixed by the
superconformal algebra~\cite{Seiberg1995,IS1996}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dim-R}
  \dim(\mathcal{O}) = \frac{3}{2}\, R(\mathcal{O})\,.
\end{equation}
The anomaly-free superconformal charge obeys
$\Nc+\Nf(R(Q)-1)=0$ with $R(\widetilde Q)=R(Q)$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R-meson}
  R(M) = 2\,R(Q) = \frac{2(\Nf - \Nc)}{\Nf}\,,
\end{equation}
so its scaling dimension at the fixed point is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dim-meson}
  \dim(M) = \frac{3(\Nf - \Nc)}{\Nf}\,.
\end{equation}
Two supercharges, each of dimension $1/2$, take the lowest component
to $F_M$.  Thus $\dim(F_M)=\dim(M)+1=3-\gamma_m$.  The SUSY
prediction for the mass anomalous dimension at the fixed point is
therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-SUSY}
  \boxed{
    \gamma_m^{\mathrm{SUSY}} = 2 - \frac{3(\Nf - \Nc)}{\Nf}
    = \frac{3\Nc - \Nf}{\Nf}\,,
    \qquad \tfrac{3}{2}\Nc < \Nf < 3\Nc\,.
  }
\end{equation}
Equivalently $\gamma_m^{\mathrm{SUSY}} = -\gamma_Q^*$ by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammaQ-star}, since $\dim(M) = 2 + \gamma_Q^*$.

% CHECK: gamma_m = 2 - 3(N_f - N_c)/N_f = (3N_c - N_f)/N_f = -gamma_Q*.
% Limits: N_f -> 3N_c gives 0 (free theory); N_f -> (3/2)N_c gives 1,
% where dim(M) = 1 saturates the unitarity bound on the gauge-invariant M.

\begin{remark}[Consistency check: conformal window boundaries]
  At the upper boundary $\Nf = 3\Nc$: $\gamma_m^{\mathrm{SUSY}} = 0$.
  The fixed point merges with the free theory, where the bilinear has
  its canonical dimension~$3$.  Approaching the lower edge
  $\Nf=\frac32\Nc$ from inside, $\gamma_m^{\mathrm{SUSY}}\to1$ and
  $\dim(F_M)\to2$.  At the edge $\dim(M)=1$ saturates the scalar
  unitarity bound.  The free chiral multiplet has a null $F$ descendant
  at separated points, so the limiting dimension does not imply a
  nonzero dimension-two correlator there.  For
  $\Nc + 2 \le \Nf \le \tfrac{3}{2}\Nc$ SQCD flows instead to a free
  magnetic phase.  The value $\gamma_m = 1$ at the lower edge agrees
  with the bound $\gamma_m \le 1$ obtained for the candidate non-SUSY
  dual of QCD with one adjoint fermion~\cite{MNPS2011,AMPS2013}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Matching the eight-flavour mass deformation}
\label{ssec:mass-map}

For $\Nc=3$, $\Nf=8$ the SQCD charges are $R(Q)=5/8$ and $R(M)=5/4$.
The dimensions and the eigenvalue $y_m$ of the mass source are therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eight-mass}
  \Delta_M=\frac{15}{8},\qquad \Delta_{F_M}=\frac{23}{8},\qquad
  y_m=4-\Delta_{F_M}=3-\Delta_M=\frac98,\qquad
  \gamma_m^*=y_m-1=\frac18 .
\end{equation}
The superspace measure in \cref{eq:mass-source} accounts for the unit
of dimension between $\phi_M$ and $F_M$.  Using $3-\Delta_M$ as a
mass anomalous dimension would instead report the source eigenvalue.

\textbf{Mass-operator matching assumption.}  In fundamental-only QCD
the complex mass matrix couples to $B^i{}_j=\bar\psi_{Rj}\psi_L^{\,i}$
and its conjugate.  For a common real mass this is
$m_R\Tr(B+B^\dagger)$.  To transfer \cref{eq:eight-mass} we must assume
that the massless eight-flavour theory reaches a fixed point at the
proposed boundary and that the leading scaling operator sourced by
$m_R$ has dimension $\Delta_{F_M}=23/8$.  In operator language the
bilinear must overlap with this eigenoperator, with no more relevant
contribution in the same channel.  The chiral flavour representation
fixes the quantum numbers of $B$.  It does not select the top or bottom
component of an SQCD meson or fix a dimension in QCD.  For example,
a leading QCD operator of dimension $15/8$ in this channel would give
$\gamma_m^*=9/8$ and a different mode-number slope, $32/17$
(\cref{eq:mode-slope}).  Neither Seiberg duality nor the magnetic
fixed point of Ref.~\cite{AMPS2013} supplies the matching.

There is a concrete obstruction to a literal multiplet identification.
In the eight-flavour theory the central element $z=-{\bf 1}_8$ of
$\SU{8}_V$ changes the sign of every quark and antiquark and leaves
every gluon invariant~\cite{Tanizaki2018}.  Thus $z=(-1)^F$ on local
gauge-invariant operators.  A local $\mathcal N=1$ supercurrent is
fermionic.  It must also be a singlet of the unbroken $\SU{8}_V$,
since a single Weyl supercharge admits only a $\UU{1}_R$ action and
$\SU{8}$ has no nontrivial one-dimensional representation~\cite{IntriligatorWecht}.
The two required signs of $z$ are incompatible.  The adjoint Weyl
fermion of the AMPS electric theory is a flavour singlet, so the
parity identity used here does not hold there.  For example the electric
composite proposed for the magnetic scalar in their Eq.~(2) contains
two adjoint fermions as well as the fundamental pair~\cite{AMPS2013}.

An independent check excludes the full eight-flavour SQCD fixed point
as an infrared endpoint of this flow.  In units where a free real
scalar contributes $1/360$ to the Euler anomaly, eight gluons and
$3\times8$ Dirac fermions give $a_{\rm UV}=(62\cdot8+11\cdot24)/360
=19/9$~\cite{Karasik2022}.  At the SQCD fixed point the eight gluinos have fermion
$R=1$ and the 48 quark Weyl fermions have $R=-3/8$.  The exact
$a_{\rm SQCD}=\tfrac{3}{32}(3\Tr R^3-\Tr R)=2535/1024$ exceeds
$a_{\rm UV}$, whereas a unitary four-dimensional RG flow requires
$a_{\rm IR}\leq a_{\rm UV}$~\cite{Anselmi1998,Komargodski2011}.
Equality of one mass exponent remains a testable, weaker hypothesis.

\subsection{Which ultraviolet theories admit a local supercurrent?}
\label{ssec:endpoint-map}

Work on flat four-dimensional spacetime in a unitary theory with a
Lorentz-invariant vacuum. Start with asymptotically free $\SU{\Nc}$
gauge fields and fundamental Dirac fermions. Normalize baryon number
$B$ so a colour baryon has $B=1$.
For $\Nc\geq3$ the connected chiral symmetry acts faithfully on
gauge-invariant local operators through
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\SU{\Nf}_L\times\SU{\Nf}_R\times\UU{1}_V}
      {\mathbb Z_{\Nf}\times\mathbb Z_{\Nc}} .
\end{equation}
Here a quark has unit $V$ charge and $V=\Nc B$. A simultaneous
flavour centre rotation on the two chiralities is cancelled by the
inverse $V$ phase. A $V$ phase of $2\pi/\Nc$ acts as a colour
centre transformation~\cite{Tanizaki2018}. For two colours the flavour
group is enhanced and the colour-centre argument below still applies.

\begin{proposition}
In the inherited local operator algebra of fundamental-only QCD
with $\Nc\geq2$, a vacuum preserving baryon number and unitary charge
conjugation $C$ cannot carry exactly one Weyl Poincar\'e supercharge.
For even $\Nc$ the local fermionic current is already excluded by
colour. For even $\Nf$ unbroken vector flavour suffices to exclude
an $\mathcal N=1$ current.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Count fundamental and antifundamental fermion insertions as $n,\bar n$.
Derivatives and gluons change neither their net charge nor parity.
Colour invariance gives $n-\bar n=\Nc B$, whence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spin-baryon}
 (-1)^F=(-1)^{n+\bar n}=(-1)^{\Nc B}.
\end{equation}
Thus even colours give only bosonic local operators. For odd colours
a fermionic current must have odd $B$. A vector-flavour singlet also
requires $\Nf\mid\Nc B$, by the phase of the flavour centre. For even
$\Nf$, its element $-\mathbf1$ acts as $(-1)^F$. A connected simple
flavour group acts trivially on the one-dimensional space of Weyl
supercharges of fixed chirality, so one can choose a singlet current.

For odd colours consider the proposed identification of baryon number
with a generator having an $R$ component. Write
$[B,\mathcal Q_\alpha]=b\mathcal Q_\alpha$. Internal, unitary $C$
commutes with the proper Lorentz group and translations, so it
preserves the undotted chirality and sends the single supercharge to
$e^{i\phi}\mathcal Q_\alpha$. It also sends $B$ to $-B$.
Conjugating the commutator gives $b=-b$. The neutral charge would
require a neutral fermionic current, in conflict with
\cref{eq:spin-baryon}.
\end{proof}

The charge-conjugation step assumes $C$ acts within the vacuum.
If $C$ exchanges broken-symmetry vacua, or several independent
supercharges of the same chirality are exchanged, that step no
longer applies. The counting concerns genuine local operators of
the microscopic theory. Endpoints of external lines do not supply
a local current acting on this Hilbert space.

\paragraph{A separate test of baryon--$R$ mixing in SQCD.}
For $\Nf\geq3$ the left and right cubic flavour anomalies of QCD are
$(\Nc,-\Nc)$ in unit fundamental normalization. They force the same
electric rank for a candidate consisting solely of SQCD with the
standard chiral flavour embedding. The mixed
$\Tr B\,\SU{\Nf}_{L,R}^2$ are $(1/2,-1/2)$, and
$\Tr B=\Tr B^3=0$. SQCD's ordinary baryon generator $B_s$ has these
same traces. Its superconformal $R_*$ instead gives
$\Tr R_*\SU{\Nf}_{L,R}^2=-\Nc^2/(2\Nf)$ for either chirality.
For an image $B=uB_s+bR_*$ the sum of the two mixed anomalies
forces $b=0$, and their difference fixes $u=1$. This argument uses
the singlet currents of isolated ordinary SQCD in its interacting
window with no extra anomaly-carrying sectors. A more general SCFT
could have extra abelian currents. The operator proof above applies
to it under the stated $C$ and single-supercharge hypotheses.

\paragraph{Euler-anomaly map.}
For $3\Nc/2<\Nf<3\Nc$, the free ultraviolet anomaly and the proposed
interacting same-rank SQCD anomaly are
\begin{align}
 a_0&=\frac{62(\Nc^2-1)+11\Nc\Nf}{360},&
 a_{\rm SQCD}&=\frac{3}{32}\left(4\Nc^2-2-\frac{6\Nc^4}{\Nf^2}\right).
 \label{eq:endpoint-a}
\end{align}
The second expression follows by tracing fermion charges $1$ for
the gluino and $-\Nc/\Nf$ for each fundamental Weyl fermion.
Adding one ultraviolet adjoint Weyl fermion without squarks gives
$a_\lambda=a_0+11(\Nc^2-1)/720$. A nontrivial flow requires a
positive difference. Both differences decrease with $x=\Nf/\Nc$
throughout the window since their slope at fixed colour is
$\Nc^2[11/360-9/(8x^3)]<0$~\cite{Anselmi1998,Komargodski2011}.
For three colours this gives

\end{center}

At six flavours the SQCD Konishi scalar
$K=Q^\dagger e^V Q+\widetilde Q^\dagger e^{-V}\widetilde Q$
is allowed by the assumed symmetries. If $\Delta_K<4$, a positive
soft deformation can drive squark decoupling. If that deformation reaches
the basin of the proposed scalar-free adjoint flow, returning to the
same SQCD point would violate strict decrease of $a$. Under this
decoupling hypothesis the candidate requires $\Delta_K\ge4$ (and a
marginal perturbation must itself be checked). Neither strong-coupling
decoupling nor the dimension has been established. A rough NSVZ estimate
using one-loop matter anomalous dimensions gives $\Delta_K\simeq23/7$.
For phases and anomalies of the adjoint-plus-fundamental theory see
Anber, Lohitsiri and Sulejmanpasic~\cite{Anber2023}.

In the theory with one massless adjoint Weyl $\lambda$ the independent
anomaly-free axial charge $X$ has $X(\lambda)=1$ and
$X(\psi_Q)=X(\psi_{\widetilde Q})=-\Nc/\Nf$. Its colour anomaly
is $\Nc+\Nf(-\Nc/\Nf)=0$. All continuous triangle traces in
$X,B,\SU{\Nf}_{L,R}$ match SQCD with $X=R_*$ because the Weyl
content and its charges agree. The adjoint gives a baryon-neutral
flavour-singlet fermion. Even the squark quantum numbers can be
realized by the colour-covariant scalar contraction
$(\lambda^a T^a)^\alpha\psi_{Q\alpha}$ and its antifundamental
counterpart. Consequently a central transformation acting trivially
on the microscopic fields up to colour also acts trivially on these
candidate squarks. These checks give kinematically allowed candidates
at five and six flavours. For the six-flavour adjoint example the
two-loop gauge coefficients are $b_0=5$ and $b_1=-22$ in the
conventions of \cref{eq:beta-2loop}. Their formal zero
$\alpha_*=10\pi/11$ is outside perturbative control. Conformality,
a conserved current, and the SQCD spectrum remain dynamical tests.

Softly broken SQCD has a third ultraviolet content: at scales above
the soft masses the squarks and gluino contribute, giving
$a_{\rm UV}^{\rm SQCD}=3(\Nc^2-1)/16+\Nc\Nf/24$.
At $(3,8)$ this is $5/2>2535/1024$. Murayama's small anomaly-mediated
deformation starts in this theory~\cite{Murayama2021}. Following it
to large soft masses requires dynamical control. AMPS find a
supersymmetric solution of perturbative magnetic coupling equations
for an adjoint-containing electric candidate~\cite{AMPS2013}.
Neither calculation establishes the six-flavour flow in the table.

\subsection{Numerical predictions for SU(3)}
\label{ssec:gamma-numerical}

For $\SU{3}$ the SQCD benchmarks in the window $4.5 < \Nf < 9$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-values}
  \gamma_m^{\mathrm{SUSY}}(\Nf) = \frac{9 - \Nf}{\Nf}
  \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
  \gamma_m^{\mathrm{SUSY}} =
  \begin{cases}
    4/5 = 0.800 & (\Nf = 5) \\
    1/2 = 0.500 & (\Nf = 6) \\
    2/7 = 0.286 & (\Nf = 7) \\
    1/8 = 0.125 & (\Nf = 8)
  \end{cases}\,.
\end{equation}
For $\Nf \geq 9$ SQCD is not asymptotically free and flows to a free
theory in the IR; formula~\eqref{eq:gamma-SUSY} would give
$\gamma_m \leq 0$ there, outside its domain.  Among the three
flavour numbers compared here, only eight lies in the interacting SQCD
window.  Whether eight is also the boundary of the fundamental-only
theory is a separate dynamical question.

These values should be compared with the non-SUSY expectations.  In the
ladder Schwinger--Dyson approximation, the lower boundary corresponds
to $\gamma_m = 1$; in the Ryttov--Sannino all-orders beta function, it
corresponds to $\gamma_m = 2$~\cite{RS2008}.  Inside the non-SUSY
conformal window, the two-loop Banks--Zaks estimate evaluates the
two-loop mass anomalous dimension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-BZ}
  \gamma_m = 6C_F\,a_* + C_F\Bigl[3C_F + \tfrac{97}{3}C_A
  - \tfrac{20}{3}T_F\Nf\Bigr]a_*^2\,,
  \qquad a_* = \frac{\alpha_*}{4\pi}\,,
\end{equation}
at the two-loop zero~\eqref{eq:alpha-BZ}, following Ryttov and
Shrock~\cite{RyttovShrock2011} ($C_F = 4/3$, $C_A = 3$, $T_F = 1/2$ for
$\SU{3}$).

\subsection{The SUSY spectrum: boson--fermion degeneracies}
\label{ssec:spectrum}

For a given flavour component of $F_M$, the scalar and pseudoscalar
densities have the same CFT dimension.  A CFT dimension governs a
correlation function.  It does not fix the masses after a relevant
deformation.  Unbroken supersymmetry in a massive theory would enforce
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma-pi}
  m_{\rm scalar}=m_{\rm pseudoscalar}
  \qquad\text{for states in the same massive chiral multiplet}.
\end{equation}
The lattice $\sigma$ is a vector-flavour singlet and the usual pion
is a nonsinglet.  A flavour-singlet supercharge preserves flavour, so
these states cannot form such a pair.  Their mass ratio is still
informative about chiral symmetry breaking.

In theories with an adjoint gluino, gauge-invariant gluonic and
gluino-bilinear interpolators occur in the components of the chiral
superfield $\Tr W^\alpha W_\alpha$.  Identifying particular poles
requires a spectral analysis, including mixing among states with the
same quantum numbers.  A theory with fundamental fermions only has
no gluino-bilinear interpolator.

%% ================================================================
%%  SECTION 4: THE FIVE DISCRIMINATING OBSERVABLES
%% ================================================================
\section{Proposed lattice observables}
\label{sec:observables}

The scalar-to-pseudoscalar ratio and the mass anomalous dimension
have direct operator definitions in the lattice theory.  For the
latter, \cref{ssec:mass-map} specifies exactly what is being compared.
The remaining three proposals depend on additional information about
the fields and symmetries of the target theory.

\subsection{Observable 1: scalar-to-pseudoscalar mass ratio}
\label{ssec:obs1}

The lightest scalar ($\sigma$, quantum numbers $0^{++}$) and
pseudoscalar ($\pi$, quantum numbers $0^{-+}$) mesons are the most
directly accessible hadronic states in lattice simulations.

\textbf{Confining (below window):}  Chiral symmetry is spontaneously
broken.  The pseudoscalar is a (pseudo-)Nambu--Goldstone boson with
$m_\pi \ll m_\sigma$.  In the chiral limit:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conf-ratio}
  \frac{m_\sigma}{m_\pi} \gg 1
  \qquad\text{(confining)}\,.
\end{equation}
In walking theories a light scalar can keep this ratio close to
unity over a finite range of fermion masses.

\textbf{At an infrared fixed point.}  Unbroken chiral flavour
symmetry can give scalar and pseudoscalar bilinears in the same chiral
representation equal dimensions even in ordinary QCD.  With a single
relevant mass source, hyperscaling gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BZ-ratio}
  m_H=A_H m_R^{1/y_m}(1+o(1)),\qquad
  \frac{m_\sigma}{m_\pi}\longrightarrow\frac{A_\sigma}{A_\pi}.
\end{equation}
The fixed-point mass exponent cancels.  The singlet scalar and
nonsinglet pion amplitudes are independent, including under the
mass-exponent matching of \cref{ssec:mass-map}.

\textbf{Lattice measurement.}  Measure the singlet scalar (including
disconnected contractions) and the nonsinglet pseudoscalar on the
same ensembles at successively smaller masses.  LatKMI observed a
scalar comparable in mass to the pion in the range it simulated
at $\Nf=8$~\cite{LatKMI2014_Nf8,LatKMI2017_Nf8}.  This motivates the
chiral extrapolation and also admits a light-dilaton interpretation.

\subsection{Observable 2: glueball--fermion-bilinear mass relation}
\label{ssec:obs2}

\textbf{Status: exploratory.}  $\SU{3}$ with fundamental flavours only
has no adjoint fermion, and the proxy below rests on a relation derived
for pure $\mathcal{N}=1$ SYM; the operator mapping is not established.

In pure $\mathcal N=1$ SYM the gluino condensate and glueball masses
are set by a common strong scale~\cite{VY1982_spectrum}.  Dimensional
analysis gives $m_{0^{++}}\sim|\langle\lambda\lambda\rangle|^{1/3}$
up to a coefficient.  It supplies no ratio between a glueball and a
fundamental-flavour scalar meson.  On fundamental-only ensembles,
gluonic and quark-bilinear operators in the singlet $0^{++}$ channel
can overlap with the same states.  A matrix of their correlators can
resolve the spectrum and overlaps.  No value of $m_{0^{++}}/m_\sigma$
follows from the mass-operator hypothesis.

\subsection{Observable 3: mass anomalous dimension}
\label{ssec:obs3}

The SQCD reference value for the mass operator in \cref{eq:mass-source} is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:obs3-SUSY}
  \boxed{
    \gamma_m = \frac{3\Nc - \Nf}{\Nf}
    = \frac{9 - \Nf}{\Nf}\quad (\SU{3},\ 4.5 < \Nf < 9)
  }
\end{equation}

\textbf{Non-SUSY conformal (Banks--Zaks):}  The two-loop estimate
exists for integer $9\leq\Nf\leq16$, outside the SUSY window, and gives $0.77$ at
$\Nf = 12$ (\cref{tab:gamma-predictions}).

\textbf{Schwinger--Dyson:}  The onset of chiral symmetry breaking
occurs at $\gamma_m = 1$.

\textbf{Dirac mode number.}  On gauge ensembles with eight dynamical
Dirac fundamentals, count the modes of the massless Dirac operator
with renormalised eigenvalue magnitude below $\Lambda_R$.  For
four-volume $V=L^3T$ and spectral density $\rho_R$ on the positive
branch, the count per unit volume, omitting exact zero modes, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode-count}
 n(\Lambda_R)=\frac{1}{V}
   \left\langle\#\{k:0<|\lambda_{k,R}|<\Lambda_R\}\right\rangle
 =2\int_0^{\Lambda_R}\rho_R(\lambda)\,d\lambda .
\end{equation}
The count is RG invariant and its threshold renormalises like the
quark mass~\cite{Patella2012,Cheng2013}.  Under coarse graining by
$b>1$, the threshold scales by $b^{y_m}$ and a four-volume by $b^{-4}$.
In the massless infinite-volume fixed-point limit this gives
$n(\Lambda_R)=b^{-4}n(b^{y_m}\Lambda_R)$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode-slope}
 p=\frac{d\log n}{d\log\Lambda_R}=\frac{4}{1+\gamma_m^*},
 \qquad
 \boxed{\ p_8=\frac{32}{9},\qquad \rho_R(\lambda)\propto\lambda^{23/9}\ }
\end{equation}
The boxed exponents assume the eight-flavour match.  Fit the slope
freely and infer $\gamma_m^*=4/p-1$.  Use thresholds
above the lowest finite-volume modes and any sea-mass distortion
($m_R\ll\Lambda_R$), below the crossover out of the IR scaling regime
and well below the lattice cutoff.  Vary the fit interval, volume,
sea mass and lattice spacing.  If counting modes of the massive
squared operator $(D+m)^\dagger(D+m)$ below $\Omega_R^2$, the
continuum threshold is $\Lambda_R=(\Omega_R^2-m_R^2)^{1/2}$.
In a finite-mass scaling interval fit the count per volume to
$n_0+A(\Omega_R^2-m_R^2)^{p/2}$, or use differences of counts to
cancel the low-mode offset $n_0$~\cite{Patella2012}.  The matched
value of $p/2$ is $16/9$.  A fixed-point slope should persist as the
continuum, infinite-volume and chiral limits expose successively lower
thresholds.  A finite
interval of slow running can give a similar effective slope.  In a
chirally broken theory the Banks--Casher density $\rho_R(0)>0$
(thermodynamic limit before the chiral limit) instead gives
$n(\Lambda_R)\sim2\rho_R(0)\Lambda_R$~\cite{BanksCasher}.

\textbf{Direct operator check.}  Use an off-diagonal flavour
component of the renormalised $B^i{}_j$ ($i\ne j$) to avoid
disconnected contractions.  With unbroken chiral flavour symmetry it
probes the same bifundamental mass channel as the trace.  At separated
Euclidean points in the IR scaling regime the proposed dimension gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bilinear-correlator}
  \langle B^i{}_j(x)(B^i{}_j)^\dagger(0)\rangle
     \propto |x|^{-2\Delta_B}=|x|^{-23/4}\,.
\end{equation}
Here $|x|$ must be short compared with the box and the correlation
length induced by the sea mass.  Single-mass hyperscaling also gives
$M_H,f_\pi\propto m_R^{1/y_m}=m_R^{8/9}$ for dimension-one spectral
quantities~\cite{DeGrand2015}.  Agreement tests the mass-channel
exponent.  A distinct limiting slope at a verified fixed point rules
out the matching assumption.  Chiral symmetry breaking would instead
invalidate the fixed-point premise.


\subsection{Observable 4: SUSY Ward identity violation}
\label{ssec:obs4}

A local $\mathcal N=1$ supercurrent $S_{\mu,R}$ would be a
renormalised, gauge-invariant fermionic operator.  At a supersymmetric
point its Ward identity can be tested at separated points with a
fermionic probe $\mathcal O$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WI-violation}
  \langle\partial^\mu S_{\mu,R}(x)\,\overline{\cal O}(0)\rangle=0,
  \qquad x\ne0 .
\end{equation}
Separated insertions avoid the contact term from the variation of
the probe.  Operator mixing and explicit breaking terms must be
accounted for in a lattice discretisation, as in simulations of pure
$\mathcal N=1$ SYM with an adjoint gluino~\cite{WardSYM}.

For eight fundamental Dirac flavours the vector-centre test in
\cref{ssec:mass-map} forbids this current in the local operator
algebra.  This Ward identity is available in theories with the
appropriate fermionic singlet, while the eight-flavour mode number in
\cref{eq:mode-count} can be measured directly in QCD.

\paragraph{A supercurrent test on adjoint-plus-six-flavour ensembles.}
Use $\SU{3}$ with six fundamental Dirac fermions, one adjoint Weyl
fermion and no elementary scalars. In this theory start from the
baryon-neutral chiral-flavour singlet
\begin{equation}
 S^{(1)}_{\mu\alpha}
 =\Tr\!\left[F_{\rho\sigma}
       (\sigma^{\rho\sigma}\sigma_\mu\bar\lambda)_\alpha\right] .
\end{equation}
It has $X=-1$ in the conventions of \cref{ssec:endpoint-map}, as a
left-handed supercharge must for $X=R_*$. Discretize $F$ with a
clover field strength, include $\Tr(F_{\mu\nu}\sigma^\nu\bar\lambda)$
and allowed operator mixing and improvements, and project the
vector-spinor onto spin $3/2$. At a superconformal point the improved
current has $\Delta_S=7/2$ and vanishing divergence and gamma trace
at separated points. Its projected two-point amplitude scales as
$|x|^{-7}$. A lattice calculation must establish nonzero overlap
and tune explicit breaking, including both sea masses~\cite{WardSYM}.

Test the normalization and action of the candidate charges on a
bosonic probe $O^i{}_j=\bar\psi_{Rj}\psi_L^i$ with $i\ne j$.
The probe has $B=0$ and is a chiral bifundamental. At six flavours
its $X=-1$ is appropriate for $F_M$. A candidate bottom-component
interpolator in the same flavour representation is
$P^i{}_j=(\psi_{\widetilde Q j}\lambda)(\lambda\psi_Q^i)$,
with each parenthesis a two-Weyl spinor contraction and the remaining
colour indices contracted. It has $X=+1$. With nonzero projections
onto the leading SQCD meson components, the separated $P P^\dagger$
and $O O^\dagger$ correlators would fall as $|x|^{-3}$ and
$|x|^{-5}$ respectively. Testing the projections requires operator
mixing and a continuum scaling interval. The Ward test can be made
on $O$ independently of these component assignments.

Define
$\delta_\alpha O$ by the flux of the renormalized $S_{\mu\alpha}$
through a closed surface about $O$, and use $\bar S$ for
$\bar\delta_{\dot\beta}$. A translation Ward identity in Euclidean
conventions, with $y$ outside both surfaces, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:translation-WI}
 \big\langle(\delta_\alpha\bar\delta_{\dot\beta}
      +\bar\delta_{\dot\beta}\delta_\alpha)O(x)\,O^\dagger(y)\big\rangle
 =2\sigma^\rho_{\alpha\dot\beta}\partial_{x^\rho}
        \langle O(x)O^\dagger(y)\rangle .
\end{equation}
The left side uses the two radially ordered, nested surface sums of the
$S\bar S O O^\dagger$ correlator. The right side is separately
measurable and nonzero. Use radii large compared with the spacing
and the crossover length to infrared scaling, and small compared with
$|x-y|$, the box and the mass-induced correlation length. Fix one
current normalization, vary the radii, repeat with a second probe
such as a gluonic scalar, and take the massless and continuum
limits with increasing volume. A zero operator passes separated-point
conservation but fails \cref{eq:translation-WI}. A dilaton effective
theory with bosonic scale and chiral symmetry alone supplies no
fermionic charges realizing this algebra. A successful Ward test
would be evidence for supersymmetry of the probed sector. Identifying
the particular SQCD fixed point additionally requires its anomalies
and spectrum, for example a chiral meson of dimension $3/2$ at six
flavours.

\subsection{Observable 5: effective potential shape and flat directions}
\label{ssec:obs5}

\textbf{Status: exploratory.}  The relation between the SUSY moduli
space and the effective potential of the lattice chiral condensate is
not established.

Define the renormalised mass density $\Sigma=\langle\bar\psi\psi\rangle$
with the ultraviolet contact terms subtracted.  Spontaneous chiral
symmetry breaking is probed by taking the infinite-volume limit before
sending the symmetry-breaking mass to zero.  At a conformal point with
one mass scaling field, dimensional scaling of the singular part gives
$V_{\rm sing}(\Sigma)\propto|\Sigma|^{4/(3-\gamma_m^*)}$ for the
corresponding scaling order parameter.

The SQCD mesonic moduli are expectation values of the lowest
component $q\tilde q$.  The top component $F_M$ is a supersymmetry
descendant and has zero expectation in a supersymmetric vacuum.
Identifying the dimension of the QCD mass density with $\Delta_{F_M}$
therefore supplies no flat direction of its effective potential.
Lattice condensate distributions and the light singlet mass can probe
chiral symmetry and a light-scalar effective theory without assuming a
modulus or a particular pseudo-modulus mass law.

\section{Comparison with existing lattice data}
\label{sec:comparison}

We now compare the predictions of~\cref{sec:predictions} with
available lattice results.  We focus on $\Nf = 8$ and $\Nf = 12$,
where substantial data exist.  Of the two, only $\Nf = 8$ lies inside
the SUSY window.

\subsection{The light scalar at eight flavours}

The LatKMI and LSD collaborations find a light flavour-singlet scalar
comparable in mass to the nonsinglet pion at the masses they have
simulated~\cite{LatKMI2014_Nf8,LSD2016_Nf8}.  Its lightness also
admits a dilaton interpretation.  Measuring its mass does not determine
the dimension of the bilinear in \cref{eq:gamma-m-def}.

LSD compared a dilaton effective theory and mass-deformed conformal
scaling on the same eight-flavour ensembles using five fermion
masses and five observables~\cite{LSD2023dilaton}. At leading order
the Akaike information criteria were $33.3$ and $60.1$ respectively.
With their selected subleading corrections the values were $24.3$
and $24.9$. The corresponding LO power $1/(1+\gamma_*)\simeq0.48$
is well below $8/9$. The NLO terms reach $46\%$ of the leading term.
Existing eight-flavour fits disfavour the $1/8$ benchmark unless
the simulated masses lie far from its scaling regime. These finite-mass fits constrain the bosonic alternatives.
The ensembles contain no adjoint fermion, which the current in
\cref{eq:translation-WI} requires.

LatKMI's channel-dependent hyperscaling fits give $\gamma_m\simeq0.6$
for $m_\pi$ and $\simeq1$ in several other channels~\cite{LatKMI2017_Nf8}.
Cheng \emph{et al.} find that the mode-number exponent depends on the
bare coupling over the scales probed~\cite{Cheng2013}.  These are
finite-range measurements.  Distinguishing walking from a limiting
value of $1/8$ requires the extrapolations in \cref{ssec:obs3}.

\subsection{Conformal fits at twelve flavours}

\textbf{Observable 3 ($\gamma_m$):}
The LatKMI collaboration reports $\gamma_m \approx 0.4$--$0.5$ for
$\Nf = 12$~\cite{LatKMI2012_Nf12}.  $\Nf = 12$ lies outside the SUSY
window ($\Nf > 3\Nc = 9$), where SQCD has no interacting fixed point,
so the SQCD exponent benchmark makes no prediction there.
The measurement tests the non-SUSY conformal scenario: the two-loop
Banks--Zaks value is $0.77$~\cite{RyttovShrock2011}, above the lattice
range, with a large truncation uncertainty
(\cref{ssec:gamma-numerical}).  If $\Nfstar$ is as high as~$12$, the
boundary lies outside the SUSY window and this benchmark does not apply.

\subsection{Ten flavours as a control outside the SQCD window}

Schr\"odinger-functional and gradient-flow step-scaling measurements
address the running coupling at ten flavours~\cite{Hayakawa2011,HRW2020}.
Under the scenario assignments in \cref{tab:scenarios}, confinement
at ten would place eight below the conformal window.  Conformality at
ten would still leave eight to be tested.

\section{Proposed measurement programme}
\label{sec:programme}

Based on the analysis above, we propose the following prioritised
measurement programme:

\subsection{Priority 1: the eight-flavour infrared limit}

Use the same ensembles for the mass-channel and chiral diagnostics.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item Measure the Dirac mode number.  Track the local slope toward
    lower thresholds and test the value $32/9$ using \cref{eq:mode-slope}.
  \item Cross-check the separated-point bilinear correlator against
    \cref{eq:bilinear-correlator}, or test single-mass hyperscaling
    with more than one spectral channel.
  \item Vary the lattice spacing, volumes and fermion masses to
    distinguish a limiting exponent from walking or a chiral
    condensate.  Test independently for a zero of the continuum
    step-scaling beta function.  Track the light scalar through
    $m_\sigma/m_\pi$ on the same ensembles.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Priority 2: SU(3) with ten flavours}

The ten-flavour theory helps locate the non-SUSY boundary.  The
following measurements complement running-coupling studies.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item Full spectrum calculation: pseudoscalar, scalar, vector,
    and axial-vector mesons.  Extract $m_\sigma / m_\pi$ in the chiral
    limit.
  \item Mass anomalous dimension $\gamma_m$ from the Dirac mode number,
    $f_\pi$ scaling, and finite-size scaling.  Compare with the SD
    value~$1$ expected at $\Nfstar$.
  \item Glueball spectrum: extract $m_{0^{++}}$ and compare with
    $m_\sigma$ (exploratory Observable~2).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparison across flavour numbers}

Compare the continuum running, Dirac spectrum and hadron masses at
eight, ten and twelve flavours.  The last two are controls for
method-dependent uncertainties and for the location of the non-SUSY
conformal window.  The eight-flavour hypothesis has its numerical
target and its rejection criterion in \cref{ssec:obs3}.

%% ================================================================
%%  SECTION 8: CONCLUSIONS
%% ================================================================
\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

In exact eight-flavour SQCD the superpotential mass couples to $F_M$
of dimension $23/8$, so
$y_m=9/8$ and $\gamma_m^*=1/8$.  In $\SU{3}$ gauge theory with only
eight Dirac fundamentals the measurable claim is that its own mass
deformation has this exponent at an IR fixed point.  The additional
operator assumption and the obstructions to interpreting it as exact
SQCD are given in \cref{ssec:mass-map}.

A continuum, chiral-limit Dirac mode-number slope of $32/9$ would
support equality of the mass exponents.  The bilinear two-point power
$23/4$ gives an independent check in position space.  A different
fixed-point slope would exclude the matching, and a nonzero limiting
Dirac spectral density would identify spontaneous chiral symmetry
breaking.  Existing finite-range exponents and a light singlet scalar
leave these infrared tests open.

For a single local supercharge in fundamental-only QCD,
\cref{ssec:endpoint-map} also excludes using baryon--$R$ mixing in a
$C$-preserving vacuum. A six-flavour theory with an additional adjoint
Weyl fermion passes the stated symmetry and Euler-anomaly tests.
The nested current-flux identity in \cref{eq:translation-WI} would
test whether its infrared charges actually generate translations.
