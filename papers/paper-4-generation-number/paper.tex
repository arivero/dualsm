\title{\textbf{Why Three Generations:\\
The $\ngen \geq 3$ Bound Across Duality Types}}
\author{Notes from the Dualised Standard Model Programme}
\date{March 2026}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The Cacciapaglia--Sannino construction identifies the Standard Model as the
magnetic (infrared) dual of an asymptotically free electric gauge theory.
In its Pati--Salam form the electric gauge group is $\SU{2\ngen - 4}$, and
requiring it to be non-abelian gives $\ngen \geq 3$.  We derive the
analogous generation bounds for the four standard families of
$\mathcal{N}=1$ Seiberg-type duality: $\SU{\Nc}$ (Seiberg), $\SO{\Nc}$
(Intriligator--Seiberg), $\Sp{\Nc}$ (Intriligator--Pouliot), and
$\SU{\Nc}$ with an adjoint (Kutasov--Schwimmer).  For each family we
write the electric gauge group as a function of~$\ngen$, impose the
non-abelian condition, and test the one-loop coefficients of both
descriptions.  One convention is used throughout: a frame counts when
$b_0^{\mathrm{el}} \geq 0$ and $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} \geq 0$, so that the
number of flavours lies inside the SUSY conformal window or on one of its
edges.  Under this convention $\ngen = 3$ is the unique value at which
both the SU and the Sp frame sit inside or on the SUSY conformal window.
The upper end of the Sp range, $\ngen \leq 3$, rests on an explicit
assumption: the magnetic $\SU{2}_L$ must have $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} \geq 0$
with supersymmetric coefficients.  With the non-supersymmetric one-loop
coefficient of the Standard Model $\SU{2}_L$ the same requirement allows
$\ngen \leq 5$, and with strict inequalities the SU and Sp ranges do not
overlap.  The SO route and Kutasov--Schwimmer duality with $k \geq 2$
also contain $\ngen = 3$, the latter on its free-magnetic edge.  All
claims about non-supersymmetric duality are conjectural.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Standard Model of particle physics contains three generations of
quarks and leptons.  No compelling explanation for this number exists
within the Standard Model itself; the generation structure is an input
rather than a prediction.  The question ``why three generations?'' has
been called one of the deepest puzzles in fundamental
physics~\cite{Frampton1992}.

A novel perspective on this question emerged from the programme of
Cacciapaglia and Sannino~\cite{CacciapagliaSannino2024,Sannino2011},
who proposed that the entire Standard Model can be understood as the
\emph{magnetic} (infrared) dual of an asymptotically free electric gauge
theory, extrapolating the pattern of Seiberg
duality~\cite{Seiberg1995} beyond supersymmetry.  In the
Pati--Salam~\cite{PS1974} form of the construction the magnetic gauge
group is $\SU{4}_{\mathrm{PS}} \supset \SU{3}_c$ and the electric gauge
group is $\SU{2\ngen - 4}$.  The requirement that this group be
non-abelian ($2\ngen - 4 \geq 2$) immediately yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CS-bound}
  \ngen \geq 3\,.
\end{equation}
If the conjectured non-supersymmetric duality
holds~\cite{AMPS2011,Sannino2011}, at least three generations are needed
for the electric theory to be a non-trivial gauge theory.

The bound relies on a specific choice of duality type ($\SU{\Nc}$
Seiberg duality in the Pati--Salam embedding).  The landscape of
$\mathcal{N}=1$ Seiberg-type dualities is richer, including:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\SU{\Nc}$ with fundamentals (Seiberg~\cite{Seiberg1995}),
  \item $\SO{\Nc}$ with vectors
    (Intriligator--Seiberg~\cite{IS9506101}),
  \item $\Sp{\Nc}$ with fundamentals
    (Intriligator--Pouliot~\cite{IP9505006}),
  \item $\SU{\Nc}$ with fundamentals and adjoint
    (Kutasov--Schwimmer~\cite{KS9505004}).
\end{itemize}
Each duality type has a different map between electric and magnetic gauge
groups, different conformal windows, and potentially different generation
bounds.

In this paper, we derive the generation bound for each of these duality
types.  The logic is uniform: identify a Standard Model gauge group
sector as the magnetic theory, use the duality map to determine the
electric gauge group as a function of~$\ngen$, require the electric group
to be non-abelian, and check the one-loop coefficients of both
descriptions against the window convention of \S\ref{ssec:convention}.
We then ask which values of $\ngen$ survive in each frame and in their
intersections.

Paper~1~\cite{Paper1} found that the $\mathrm{SU}$ (Cacciapaglia--Sannino) and
$\mathrm{Sp}$ routes overlap only at $\ngen = 3$, once the edges of the
conformal window are counted and the magnetic $\SU{2}_L$ is required to
have $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} \geq 0$.  Here we add the $\mathrm{SO}$ and
Kutasov--Schwimmer dualities, state the window convention once, and list
the assumptions behind the result in Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}.

\medskip

\textbf{Epistemic convention.}  The SUSY Seiberg-type dualities are
supported by strong evidence in $\mathcal{N}=1$ supersymmetric gauge
theory ('t~Hooft anomaly matching, moduli spaces, deformations).  All
claims extrapolating these dualities to the non-supersymmetric Standard
Model are \textbf{conjectural} and carry an implicit qualifier ``if the
non-SUSY duality holds.''  Once the embedding and the window convention
are fixed, the generation bounds follow from group theory and one-loop
coefficients; their physical relevance to the Standard Model depends on
the dynamical assumption that such dualities extend beyond SUSY.

\medskip

\textbf{Summary of results.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The $\mathrm{SU}$ Seiberg (direct QCD) route gives
    $\ngen \in \{3, 4\}$; at $\ngen = 3$ it is the Cacciapaglia--Sannino
    QCD-sector duality and sits inside the window.  The $\mathrm{SU}$
    Pati--Salam route gives $\ngen \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$, with $\ngen = 3$
    on the upper (electric) edge (\S\ref{sec:SU-duality}).
  \item The $\mathrm{Sp}$ route gives $\ngen \geq 2$ from group theory and
    $\ngen \leq 3$ from $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}}(\SU{2}_L) \geq 0$, hence
    $2 \leq \ngen \leq 3$, with $\ngen = 3$ on the lower (free-magnetic)
    edge (\S\ref{sec:Sp-duality}).
  \item The $\mathrm{SO}$ route gives $\ngen \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$, with
    $\ngen = 3$ on the upper edge, the same kind of edge as the
    Pati--Salam route at $\ngen = 3$ (\S\ref{sec:SO-duality}).
  \item Kutasov--Schwimmer duality with $k \geq 2$ gives $\ngen \leq 3$
    for every $k$, with $\ngen = 3$ on the free-magnetic edge; $k = 1$
    reduces to the $\mathrm{SU}$ Seiberg route (\S\ref{sec:KS-duality}).
  \item Under the inclusive convention, $\ngen = 3$ is the unique value
    at which both the SU and the Sp frame sit inside or on the SUSY
    conformal window, and it lies in the range of every route.  Strict
    inequalities empty the SU--Sp overlap (\S\ref{sec:master-table}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Framework and Conventions}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{The duality programme}

We follow the framework of Cacciapaglia and
Sannino~\cite{CacciapagliaSannino2024}: a Standard Model gauge group
factor $G_{\SM}$ is identified as the \emph{magnetic} gauge group of a
Seiberg-type duality, and the electric theory provides the UV completion.
The matter content of the magnetic theory is fixed by the Standard Model
spectrum, which determines the number of flavours~$\Nf$ as a function
of the generation number~$\ngen$.

The generation bound arises from two constraints:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Non-abelian condition:} The electric gauge group must be
    non-abelian (\ie, non-trivial as a gauge theory).  For $\SU{N}$,
    this means $N \geq 2$; for $\SO{N}$, $N \geq 3$; for $\Sp{N}$,
    $N \geq 1$ (where $\Sp{N}$ has rank~$N$).
  \item \textbf{Window condition:} The one-loop coefficients of both
    descriptions must be non-negative, as stated in
    \S\ref{ssec:convention}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Window convention}
\label{ssec:convention}

We use one convention for every duality family.  With $\mathcal{N}=1$
one-loop coefficients $b_0 = 3h - \sum_r T(r)$ ($h$ the dual Coxeter
number of the gauge group, $T(r)$ the Dynkin index of each chiral
multiplet), a frame \textbf{counts} when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convention}
  b_0^{\mathrm{el}} \geq 0
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} \geq 0\,,
\end{equation}
where $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}}$ refers to the magnetic gauge group, \ie\ the
Standard Model factor.  For $\mathrm{SU}$, $\mathrm{Sp}$ and $\mathrm{SO}$ duality the edge
$b_0^{\mathrm{el}} = 0$ is the upper edge of the SUSY conformal window and
$b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} = 0$ is its lower edge, so~\eqref{eq:convention} says
that $\Nf$ lies inside the window or on one of its edges.  Two remarks
make the content of the convention explicit:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Edges are included.}  On an edge the one-loop coefficient
    of one description vanishes and that description is infrared free,
    so the edge carries no interacting fixed point.  We count the edges
    because the strict convention ($b_0^{\mathrm{el}} > 0$ and
    $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} > 0$) leaves no overlap between the SU and Sp
    frames (\S\ref{ssec:intersection}); the result depends on this
    choice.
  \item \textbf{The free magnetic phase is excluded.}  For
    $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} < 0$ the magnetic theory is infrared free and,
    in SUSY, it is the correct description of the infrared: the duality
    still holds there~\cite{IS1995}.  Requiring
    $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} \geq 0$ is therefore an assumption about the
    Standard Model factor: it must be asymptotically free or marginal as
    a magnetic theory.  Sannino's upper bound $\ngen \leq 6$ in the
    Pati--Salam route uses the same requirement~\cite{Sannino2011}.  For
    the magnetic $\SU{2}_L$ it is the only source of the upper bound
    $\ngen \leq 3$ (assumption~A4 in Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conventions}

We follow the conventions of Seiberg~\cite{Seiberg1995} and
Intriligator--Seiberg~\cite{IS9506101,IS1995}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Sp{\Nc}$ denotes the symplectic group of rank~$\Nc$, with
    fundamental representation of dimension~$2\Nc$.  Thus
    $\Sp{1} = \SU{2}$.
  \item $\SO{\Nc}$ has an $\Nc$-dimensional vector representation.
  \item Dynkin indices: $T(\mathrm{fund}\;\SU{N}) = \tfrac{1}{2}$,
    $T(\mathrm{vector}\;\SO{N}) = 1$,
    $T(\mathrm{fund}\;\Sp{N}) = \tfrac{1}{2}$.
  \item Dual Coxeter numbers: $h(\SU{N}) = N$, $h(\SO{N}) = N - 2$,
    $h(\Sp{N}) = N + 1$.
  \item $\Nf$ counts Dirac flavour pairs for SU duality, vector fields
    for SO duality, and $2\Nf$ total fundamentals for Sp duality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{SM matter content as a function of $\ngen$}
\label{ssec:SM-matter}

The Standard Model with $\ngen$ generations contains:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{QCD sector ($\SU{3}_c$):} $2\ngen$ Dirac quarks
    (each generation contributes one up-type and one down-type quark),
    hence $\Nf^{\mathrm{QCD}} = 2\ngen$.
  \item \textbf{EW sector ($\SU{2}_L$):} 4~Weyl doublets per
    generation (3~quark doublets from colour $+$ 1~lepton doublet),
    $4\ngen$ in total, hence $2\Nf^{\mathrm{EW}} = 4\ngen$, giving
    $\Nf^{\mathrm{EW}} = 2\ngen$ in the Sp counting convention.
  \item \textbf{Pati--Salam ($\SU{4}_{\mathrm{PS}}$):}
    $2\ngen$ Dirac flavours in the fundamental of $\SU{4}_{\mathrm{PS}}$;
    the magnetic group now has rank~4 instead of~3, which lowers the
    electric rank by one (see \S\ref{ssec:pati-salam}).
\end{itemize}

\section{$\SU{\Nc}$ Seiberg Duality}
\label{sec:SU-duality}

\subsection{Seiberg duality map}

The electric theory is $\mathcal{N}=1$ $\SU{\Nc}$ SQCD with $\Nf$
fundamental flavours.  The magnetic dual is $\SU{\Nf - \Nc}$ with $\Nf$
dual quarks and an $\Nf \times \Nf$ meson
matrix~\cite{Seiberg1995}.

The conformal window is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SU-CW}
  \frac{3}{2}\,\Nc < \Nf < 3\Nc\,,
\end{equation}
with lower edge $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} = 3(\Nf - \Nc) - \Nf = 0$ and upper
edge $b_0^{\mathrm{el}} = 3\Nc - \Nf = 0$.  Under the convention of
\S\ref{ssec:convention} both edges are included.

\subsection{Direct QCD route}
\label{ssec:direct-QCD}

We identify $\SU{3}_c$ as the magnetic gauge group:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SU-magnetic}
  \Nf - \Nc = 3
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \Nc = \Nf - 3\,.
\end{equation}
The number of QCD Dirac flavours is $\Nf = 2\ngen$
(\cf~\S\ref{ssec:SM-matter}).  The electric gauge group is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SU-electric}
  \boxed{\SU{2\ngen - 3}}\,.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Non-abelian condition.}
$2\ngen - 3 \geq 2$ requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SU-bound}
  \boxed{\ngen \geq \frac{5}{2}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \ngen \geq 3}\,.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Conformal window.}
From~\eqref{eq:SU-CW} with $\Nc = 2\ngen - 3$, edges included:
\begin{align}
  \text{Lower:}&\quad \frac{3(2\ngen - 3)}{2} \leq 2\ngen
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  6\ngen - 9 \leq 4\ngen
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \ngen \leq \frac{9}{2}\,,
  \label{eq:SU-CW-lower} \\
  \text{Upper:}&\quad 2\ngen \leq 3(2\ngen - 3) = 6\ngen - 9
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \ngen \geq \frac{9}{4}\,.
  \label{eq:SU-CW-upper}
\end{align}
So $\frac{9}{4} \leq \ngen \leq \frac{9}{2}$; both edges are
non-integer, so strict and inclusive counting agree:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SU-CW-ng}
  \ngen \in \{3, 4\}\,.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Numerical check ($\ngen = 3$).}
$\Nc = 3$, $\Nf = 6$: electric $\SU{3}$ with 6~flavours and magnetic
$\SU{3}_c$, the QCD-sector duality of
Cacciapaglia--Sannino~\cite{CacciapagliaSannino2024}.  Conformal window
$4.5 < 6 < 9$, with $b_0^{\mathrm{el}} = b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} = 3$.
\checkmark

\subsection{Pati--Salam route (Cacciapaglia--Sannino)}
\label{ssec:pati-salam}

In the Pati--Salam
embedding~\cite{PS1974,Sannino2011,CacciapagliaSannino2024}, the
electric theory is an $\SU{N}$ gauge theory with $\Nf$ Dirac
fundamentals \emph{and} one adjoint Weyl fermion~$\lambda$, the
counterpart of the gaugino of SQCD.  The magnetic gauge group is
$\SU{4}_{\mathrm{PS}}$, which contains $\SU{3}_c$, with the ``fourth
colour'' corresponding to lepton number.  Setting $\Nf - \Nc = 4$ with
$\Nf = 2\ngen$ gives the electric gauge group
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PS-electric}
  \boxed{\SU{2\ngen - 4}}
\end{equation}
(see~\cite{Sannino2011,CacciapagliaSannino2024} and Lecture~7 of the
accompanying notes).

\paragraph{Non-abelian condition.}
$2\ngen - 4 \geq 2$ requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PS-bound}
  \boxed{\ngen \geq 3}\,.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Conformal window.}
In the supersymmetric counterpart the adjoint fermion~$\lambda$ is the
gaugino, so the electric theory is SQCD with
$b_0^{\mathrm{el}} = 3\Nc - \Nf$ and the window is~\eqref{eq:SU-CW}.
(An additional adjoint \emph{chiral} multiplet would give
$2\Nc - \Nf$; that is the Kutasov--Schwimmer setting of
\S\ref{sec:KS-duality}.)  With $\Nc = 2\ngen - 4$ and $\Nf = 2\ngen$,
edges included:
\begin{align}
  \text{Lower:}&\quad 3(2\ngen - 4)/2 \leq 2\ngen
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  3\ngen - 6 \leq 2\ngen
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \ngen \leq 6\,,
  \label{eq:PS-CW-lower} \\
  \text{Upper:}&\quad 2\ngen \leq 3(2\ngen - 4) = 6\ngen - 12
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \ngen \geq 3\,.
  \label{eq:PS-CW-upper}
\end{align}
Thus $3 \leq \ngen \leq 6$: $\ngen \in \{4, 5\}$ lie strictly inside the
window and $\ngen = 3, 6$ lie on its edges.

For $\ngen = 3$: $\Nc = 2$, $\Nf = 6 = 3\Nc$, so
$b_0^{\mathrm{el}} = 0$ while the magnetic $\SU{4}_{\mathrm{PS}}$ has
$b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} = 12 - 6 = 6$.  The frame sits on the upper
(electric) edge, where the electric theory is infrared free, and the
inclusive convention counts it.  For $\ngen = 6$ it sits on the lower
edge ($b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} = 0$).  Sannino's range
$3 \leq \ngen \leq 6$~\cite{Sannino2011} follows from the same two
conditions, a non-abelian electric group and $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} \geq 0$.

Combined PS constraint (non-abelian + window):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PS-combined}
  3 \leq \ngen \leq 6\,.
\end{equation}

\section{$\Sp{\Nc}$ Duality}
\label{sec:Sp-duality}

\subsection{Sp duality map}

The electric theory is $\mathcal{N}=1$ $\Sp{\Nc}$ with $2\Nf$
fundamentals~\cite{IP9505006,IS1995}.  The magnetic dual is
$\Sp{\Nf - \Nc - 2}$ with $2\Nf$ dual fundamentals and an antisymmetric
meson.

The conformal window is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sp-CW}
  \frac{3(\Nc + 1)}{2} < \Nf < 3(\Nc + 1)\,,
\end{equation}
with lower edge $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} = 3(\Nf - \Nc - 1) - \Nf = 0$ and
upper edge $b_0^{\mathrm{el}} = 3(\Nc + 1) - \Nf = 0$.

\subsection{EW sector: $\SU{2}_L = \Sp{1}$ as magnetic}

We identify $\SU{2}_L \cong \Sp{1}$ as the magnetic gauge group
(following~\cite{Paper1}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sp-constraint}
  \Nf - \Nc - 2 = 1
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \Nc = \Nf - 3\,.
\end{equation}

The electroweak sector contains $2\Nf = 4\ngen$ Weyl doublets (see
\S\ref{ssec:SM-matter}), identified with the $2\Nf$ dual quarks of the
magnetic $\Sp{1}$, giving $\Nf = 2\ngen$.
The electric gauge group is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sp-electric}
  \boxed{\Sp{2\ngen - 3}}\,.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Non-abelian condition.}
$\Sp{\Nc}$ requires $\Nc \geq 1$, so $2\ngen - 3 \geq 1$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sp-bound}
  \boxed{\ngen \geq 2}\,.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Conformal window.}
From~\eqref{eq:Sp-CW} with $\Nc = 2\ngen - 3$, edges included:
\begin{align}
  \text{Lower:}&\quad \frac{3(2\ngen - 2)}{2} \leq 2\ngen
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  3\ngen - 3 \leq 2\ngen
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \ngen \leq 3\,,
  \label{eq:Sp-CW-lower} \\
  \text{Upper:}&\quad 2\ngen \leq 3(2\ngen - 2) = 6\ngen - 6
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \ngen \geq \frac{3}{2}\,.
  \label{eq:Sp-CW-upper}
\end{align}
The lower-edge condition is
$b_0^{\mathrm{mag}}(\SU{2}_L) = 3 \cdot 2 - \Nf = 6 - 2\ngen \geq 0$.
Only $\ngen = 2$ lies strictly inside the window.

For $\ngen = 3$: $\Nc = 3$ and $\Nf = 6 = \frac{3}{2}(3 + 1)$, so
$\ngen = 3$ sits \emph{on the lower edge} of the window:
$b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} = 0$, the magnetic $\SU{2}_L$ coupling is marginal at
one loop, and the frame sits at the boundary of the free magnetic phase,
while the electric $\Sp{3}$ is asymptotically free
($b_0^{\mathrm{el}} = 6$).  For $\ngen \geq 4$ the frame lies in the free
magnetic phase ($b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} < 0$), which in SUSY is a valid dual
description; only the requirement $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} \geq 0$
(assumption~A4 in Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}) removes it.

Under that assumption the combined Sp constraint is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sp-combined}
  \boxed{2 \leq \ngen \leq 3}\,.
\end{equation}

\section{$\SO{\Nc}$ Duality}
\label{sec:SO-duality}

\subsection{SO duality map}

The electric theory is $\mathcal{N}=1$ $\SO{\Nc}$ with $\Nf$
vectors~\cite{IS9506101}.  The magnetic dual is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SO-dual}
  \SO{\Nf - \Nc + 4}
\end{equation}
with $\Nf$ dual vectors and a symmetric meson.  Note the crucial
$+4$ shift, which distinguishes SO from SU duality.

The conformal window is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SO-CW}
  \frac{3(\Nc - 2)}{2} < \Nf < 3(\Nc - 2)\,.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Embedding: $\SO{6} \cong \SU{4}_{\mathrm{PS}}$ as magnetic}

The Standard Model gauge groups are $\SU{3}_c \times \SU{2}_L
\times \UU{1}_Y$.  None of these is an SO group.  However, in the
Pati--Salam framework, $\SU{3}_c$ extends to $\SU{4}_{\mathrm{PS}}$,
and we may use the local isomorphism
$\SO{6} \cong \SU{4}$ to embed the Pati--Salam sector in an SO
duality~\cite{IS9506101,PS1974}.

The 6-dimensional vector of $\SO{6}$ decomposes under
$\SU{3}_c \subset \SU{4}$ as $\mathbf{3} \oplus \overline{\mathbf{3}}$.
Each vector therefore accommodates one quark plus one antiquark.  With
$2\ngen$ Dirac quarks in the Standard Model, we need
\begin{equation}
  \Nf = 2\ngen
\end{equation}
vectors of $\SO{6}$.

Setting the magnetic gauge group to $\SO{6}$:
\begin{equation}
  \Nf - \Nc + 4 = 6
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \Nc = \Nf - 2 = 2\ngen - 2\,.
\end{equation}
The electric gauge group is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SO-electric}
  \boxed{\SO{2\ngen - 2}}\,.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Non-abelian condition.}
$\SO{\Nc}$ is non-abelian for $\Nc \geq 3$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SO-bound}
  2\ngen - 2 \geq 3
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \boxed{\ngen \geq \frac{5}{2} \;\Longrightarrow\; \ngen \geq 3}\,.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Conformal window.}
From~\eqref{eq:SO-CW} with $\Nc = 2\ngen - 2$, edges included:
\begin{align}
  \text{Lower:}&\quad \frac{3(2\ngen - 4)}{2} \leq 2\ngen
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  3\ngen - 6 \leq 2\ngen
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \ngen \leq 6\,,
  \label{eq:SO-CW-lower} \\
  \text{Upper:}&\quad 2\ngen \leq 3(2\ngen - 4) = 6\ngen - 12
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \ngen \geq 3\,.
  \label{eq:SO-CW-upper}
\end{align}
So $3 \leq \ngen \leq 6$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SO-CW-ng}
  \ngen \in \{4, 5\} \quad\text{(strictly inside)}\,,
  \qquad
  \ngen \in \{3, 6\} \quad\text{(on the edges)}\,.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The $\ngen = 3$ case.}
For $\ngen = 3$: $\Nc = 4$, $\Nf = 6$, and
\begin{equation}
  b_0^{\mathrm{el}} = 3(\Nc - 2) - \Nf = 3(2) - 6 = 0\,,
  \qquad
  b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} = 3(6 - 2) - 6 = 6\,.
\end{equation}
The electric $\SO{4}$ theory with 6~vectors sits on the upper (electric)
edge of the window, exactly as the Pati--Salam route does at $\ngen = 3$
(\S\ref{ssec:pati-salam}).  Any uniform convention treats the two cases
alike; the inclusive one counts both.  $\SO{4} \cong \SU{2} \times \SU{2}$
is semisimple rather than simple, so the $\ngen = 3$ electric theory is a
product-group theory; we apply the same window formula to it.

\paragraph{Combined SO constraint.}
The non-abelian condition gives $\ngen \geq 3$ and the window gives
$3 \leq \ngen \leq 6$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SO-combined}
  \boxed{3 \leq \ngen \leq 6 \quad\text{(SO route)}}\,.
\end{equation}
The SO route therefore contains $\ngen = 3$ under the inclusive
convention.  The strict convention gives $\ngen \in \{4, 5\}$, and it
also removes $\ngen = 3$ from the Pati--Salam and Sp routes
(\S\ref{ssec:intersection}).

\section{Kutasov--Schwimmer Duality}
\label{sec:KS-duality}

\subsection{KS duality map}

The electric theory is $\mathcal{N}=1$ $\SU{\Nc}$ with $\Nf$
fundamentals, one adjoint~$\Phi$, and superpotential
$W = \frac{g}{k+1}\Tr(\Phi^{k+1})$, where $k \geq 1$ is a positive
integer~\cite{KS9505004,KSS9507040}.

The magnetic dual is $\SU{k\Nf - \Nc}$ with $\Nf$ dual quarks, one
dual adjoint~$\phi$, and $k$~meson species $M_j \sim Q\Phi^j
\widetilde{Q}$ ($j = 0, \ldots, k-1$).

For $k = 1$ the superpotential is a mass term for~$\Phi$; integrating
$\Phi$ out gives SQCD and the Seiberg window~\eqref{eq:SU-CW}.  For
$k \geq 2$ the adjoint stays massless in both descriptions, and the
one-loop coefficients are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KS-b0}
  b_0^{\mathrm{el}} = 3\Nc - \Nc - \Nf = 2\Nc - \Nf\,,
  \qquad
  b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} = 2(k\Nf - \Nc) - \Nf = (2k - 1)\Nf - 2\Nc\,.
\end{equation}
The electric theory is asymptotically free for $\Nf < 2\Nc$, the magnetic
theory is infrared free (free magnetic phase) for $\Nf < 2\Nc/(2k - 1)$,
and stability requires $\Nf \geq \Nc/k$, so that
$k\Nf - \Nc \geq 0$~\cite{KS9505004,KSS9507040}.  The window defined by
the convention~\eqref{eq:convention} is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KS-CW}
  \frac{2\Nc}{2k - 1} \leq \Nf \leq 2\Nc
  \qquad (k \geq 2)\,.
\end{equation}
Two further conditions stay outside this count.  The unitarity bound on
the meson $M_0 = Q\widetilde{Q}$, whose $R$-charge is fixed by
$R(\Phi) = 2/(k+1)$, requires $\Nf \geq 3\Nc/(k+1)$; below that value
$M_0$ decouples as a free field while the rest of the theory can stay
interacting, so the bound marks a decoupling point inside the
interacting range.  For $k \geq 3$ the fixed point also requires
$\Tr(\Phi^{k+1})$ to be relevant at the $W = 0$ fixed point, which
$a$-maximization confirms only for large enough
$\Nc/\Nf$~\cite{IW2003}.

\subsection{Generation bound as a function of $k$}

We identify $\SU{3}_c$ as the magnetic gauge group for the QCD sector:
\begin{equation}
  k\Nf - \Nc = 3
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \Nc = k\Nf - 3 = 2k\ngen - 3\,,
\end{equation}
using $\Nf = 2\ngen$.

The electric gauge group is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KS-electric}
  \SU{2k\ngen - 3}\,.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Non-abelian condition.}
$2k\ngen - 3 \geq 2$ requires $\ngen \geq \frac{5}{2k}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KS-bound}
  \ngen \geq \left\lceil \frac{5}{2k} \right\rceil
  \;=\; \begin{cases}
    3 & k = 1\,, \\
    2 & k = 2\,, \\
    1 & k \geq 3\,.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Window ($k \geq 2$).}
Substituting $\Nc = 2k\ngen - 3$ and $\Nf = 2\ngen$
into~\eqref{eq:KS-b0}:
\begin{align}
  b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} &= (2k - 1)\,2\ngen - 2(2k\ngen - 3)
  = 6 - 2\ngen \;\geq\; 0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \ngen \leq 3\,,
  \label{eq:KS-lower} \\
  b_0^{\mathrm{el}} &= 2(2k\ngen - 3) - 2\ngen
  = (4k - 2)\,\ngen - 6 \;\geq\; 0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \ngen \geq \frac{3}{2k - 1}\,.
  \label{eq:KS-upper}
\end{align}
The magnetic coefficient is independent of~$k$ because the magnetic
theory is always $\SU{3}_c$ with one adjoint and $2\ngen$ flavours.  The
window in the $\ngen$ variable is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KS-CW-ng}
  \frac{3}{2k - 1} \leq \ngen \leq 3
  \qquad (k \geq 2)\,,
\end{equation}
and $\ngen = 3$ sits on its lower (free-magnetic) edge for every
$k \geq 2$.  Stability, $k\Nf - \Nc = 3 > 0$, holds automatically.

\end{center}

For $k \geq 2$ the window contains $\ngen = 3$ on its edge, with the same

Kutasov--Schwimmer duality with $k \geq 2$ therefore gives a second upper

$b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} \geq 0$; for $k \geq 3$ it also admits $\ngen = 1$,
subject to the relevance condition above.

\begin{remark}[Earlier window]
An earlier version of this analysis used the window
$3\Nc/(k+1) < \Nf < 3\Nc/k$, which admits no integer $\ngen$ for
$k \geq 2$.  Its lower edge is the $M_0$ unitarity bound discussed above,
and its upper edge follows from neither one-loop coefficient: for $k = 2$
it cuts off the range $3\Nc/2 < \Nf < 2\Nc$, where both descriptions are
asymptotically free.  The window~\eqref{eq:KS-CW} uses the same edges as
the SU, Sp and SO windows.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Intersection of the SU and Sp frames}
\label{ssec:intersection}

The Standard Model gauge groups are $\SU{3}_c$, $\SU{2}_L \cong \Sp{1}$
and $\UU{1}_Y$, so the frames that dualise a Standard Model factor
directly are the SU frames (magnetic $\SU{3}_c$, or
$\SU{4}_{\mathrm{PS}} \supset \SU{3}_c$) and the Sp frame (magnetic
$\SU{2}_L$).  Under the inclusive convention:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intersection}
  \Bigl(\,\underbrace{\ngen \in \{3, 4\}}_{\text{SU Seiberg}}
  \;\text{or}\;
  \underbrace{\ngen \in \{3, \ldots, 6\}}_{\text{SU Pati--Salam}}\,\Bigr)
  \;\;\cap\;\;
  \underbrace{2 \leq \ngen \leq 3}_{\text{Sp route}}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \boxed{\ngen = 3}\,.
\end{equation}

This is the central result of this paper: \textbf{under the inclusive
convention~\eqref{eq:convention}, $\ngen = 3$ is the unique value at which
both the SU and the Sp frame sit inside or on the SUSY conformal window.}
It rests on the assumptions of Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}, two of which
carry the result:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Edges.}  With strict inequalities the SU Seiberg range
    stays $\{3, 4\}$, the Pati--Salam range becomes $\{4, 5\}$ and the
    Sp range becomes $\{2\}$, so the SU--Sp overlap is empty.  The value
    $\ngen = 3$ survives because the edges are counted: the Sp frame sits
    on its lower edge at $\ngen = 3$.
  \item \textbf{Magnetic $\SU{2}_L$.}  The upper end of the Sp range,
    $\ngen \leq 3$, comes from the requirement
    $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}}(\SU{2}_L) \geq 0$ alone
    (\S\ref{ssec:robustness}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Role of the SO and Kutasov--Schwimmer routes}
\label{ssec:SO-KS-role}

Under the same convention the SO route gives $3 \leq \ngen \leq 6$ and
Kutasov--Schwimmer duality with $k \geq 2$ gives $\ngen \leq 3$, so every
route in Table~\ref{tab:master} contains $\ngen = 3$.  At $\ngen = 3$ the
SO frame sits on the upper edge, like the Pati--Salam frame, and the
Kutasov--Schwimmer frames sit on the lower edge, like the Sp frame.
These routes are consistent with the central result and add no
independent selection of $\ngen = 3$.

The Kutasov--Schwimmer entries depend on the choice of lower edge.  The
narrower window $3\Nc/(k+1) < \Nf < 2\Nc$, in which every meson
interacts (the convention of Lecture~9 and Exercise Set~7 of the
accompanying course), puts $\ngen = 3$ outside for every $k \geq 2$: at
$k = 2$, $\Nf = 6$ lies below $3\Nc/(k+1) = 9$.  For the SU, Sp and SO
frames the two lower edges coincide, because the meson reaches the
unitarity bound exactly where $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}} = 0$, so the central
result holds under either choice.

\subsection{Robustness of the $\ngen = 3$ selection}
\label{ssec:robustness}

The result $\ngen = 3$ depends on:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The SU~route lower bound $\ngen \geq 3$ (from requiring
    $\SU{2\ngen - 3}$ or $\SU{2\ngen - 4}$ to be non-abelian).
  \item The Sp~route upper bound $\ngen \leq 3$, which is the condition
    $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}}(\SU{2}_L) = 6 - 2\ngen \geq 0$ with the
    supersymmetric coefficient.
\end{enumerate}
The lower bound is purely group-theoretic and does not depend on
dynamical assumptions.  The upper bound depends on two choices.  First,
it excludes the free magnetic phase ($\ngen \geq 4$), which in SUSY is a
valid dual description; requiring the magnetic $\SU{2}_L$ to be
asymptotically free or marginal is an assumption about the Standard
Model, motivated by the asymptotic freedom of the observed $\SU{2}_L$.
Second, it uses the supersymmetric coefficient, which counts the gaugino
and the scalar partners of the doublets.  The non-supersymmetric one-loop
coefficient of the Standard Model $\SU{2}_L$ with $4\ngen$ Weyl doublets
and $n_H$ Higgs doublets,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SM-b0}
  b_0^{\SM}(\SU{2}_L) = \frac{22}{3} - \frac{4\ngen}{3} - \frac{n_H}{6}\,,
\end{equation}
stays positive up to $\ngen = 5$ for $n_H = 0$ or~$1$ (it equals $19/6$
at $\ngen = 3$, $n_H = 1$).  With that coefficient the same requirement
gives only $\ngen \leq 5$.  The upper bound $\ngen \leq 3$ therefore also
assumes that the supersymmetric coefficient governs the magnetic
$\SU{2}_L$, as it would in a quasi-supersymmetric magnetic spectrum above
the superpartner scale.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What selects $\ngen = 3$}

Under the convention~\eqref{eq:convention}, $\ngen = 3$ is the unique
number of generations at which both the SU and the Sp frame sit inside
or on the SUSY conformal window.  The result can be understood as
follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \emph{SU~route} provides a lower bound ($\ngen \geq 3$) because
    too few generations would make the electric gauge group abelian or
    trivial.
  \item The \emph{Sp~route} provides an upper bound ($\ngen \leq 3$)
    through the requirement $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}}(\SU{2}_L) \geq 0$.  For
    $\ngen \geq 4$ the frame lies in the free magnetic phase, where the
    duality still holds in SUSY and the magnetic $\SU{2}_L$ is infrared
    free; excluding that phase is assumption~A4.
  \item These two constraints ``squeeze'' from opposite sides, leaving
    $\ngen = 3$, which the Sp frame reaches on its edge.
\end{itemize}

The SO route and Kutasov--Schwimmer duality with $k \geq 2$ both contain
$\ngen = 3$ under the same convention, so they are compatible with this
picture and leave it unchanged.

\subsection{Model dependence}

The generation bounds depend on how the Standard Model matter content
is embedded in the duality framework:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Flavour counting:} The number of ``flavours'' $\Nf$
    depends on whether we count quarks only (direct QCD route) or
    quarks and leptons together (Pati--Salam route).  Both choices give
    $\ngen \geq 3$ for the SU route.
  \item \textbf{Embedding of $\SU{2}_L$:} The Sp~route identifies all
    $4\ngen$ $\SU{2}_L$ doublets (quarks and leptons) with the $2\Nf$
    dual quarks of the magnetic $\Sp{1}$.
  \item \textbf{Extension to non-SUSY:} The window edges hold in the
    SUSY theory.  Their non-SUSY extrapolation can move the upper bound:
    with the Standard Model coefficient~\eqref{eq:SM-b0} the requirement
    $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}}(\SU{2}_L) \geq 0$ allows $\ngen \leq 5$
    (\S\ref{ssec:robustness}).  The SU lower bound is unaffected.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparison with other generation-number arguments}

Previous arguments for $\ngen = 3$ include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Anomaly cancellation:} The SM is anomaly-free for any
    $\ngen$.  In $\SU{3}_c \times \SU{3}_L \times \UU{1}_X$ extensions,
    anomaly cancellation requires $\ngen$ to be a multiple of the number
    of colours, and asymptotic freedom of QCD then selects
    $\ngen = 3$~\cite{Frampton1992}.
  \item \textbf{CP violation:} The CKM matrix has a physical phase only
    for $\ngen \geq 3$, but this does not explain \emph{why} the phase
    should be nonzero.
  \item \textbf{Asymptotic freedom of QCD:} $b_0 = 11 - 2\Nf/3 > 0$
    requires $\Nf < 16.5$, \ie, $\ngen \leq 8$.  This is an upper bound
    but a weak one.
  \item \textbf{Cosmological and precision constraints:}
    Limits from $Z$-boson width, BBN, and CMB give $\ngen = 3$ for
    light neutrinos, but these are experimental measurements, not
    theoretical explanations.
\end{itemize}

The duality argument relates $\ngen$ to the consistency of identifying
Standard Model factors as magnetic duals, without reference to specific
experimental data.  It rests on the assumptions of
Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}, above all the conjecture that Seiberg-type
dualities extend to non-supersymmetric theories.

\subsection{Testability}

The duality framework makes additional predictions beyond the generation
number:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Composite Higgs:} In the SU~route, the Higgs is a
    composite meson with specific quantum numbers and coupling
    structure~\cite{CacciapagliaSannino2024}.
  \item \textbf{Scalar diquarks and leptoquarks:} In the Sp~route, the
    meson content includes colour-triplet scalars that could be
    observable at colliders~\cite{Paper1}.
  \item \textbf{Conformal window edges:} For $\ngen = 3$ the Sp and
    Pati--Salam frames sit on edges of the window and the SU Seiberg
    frame sits inside it.  Lattice studies of theories in this regime
    could test the existence of the IR fixed point.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have derived the generation bound $\ngen$ for the Standard Model
across the classical-group families of $\mathcal{N}=1$ Seiberg-type
duality, with window edges included
(convention~\eqref{eq:convention}).  Our results are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{SU Seiberg duality} ($\SU{3}_c$ as magnetic):
    electric $\SU{2\ngen - 3}$, bound $\ngen \geq 3$, window-compatible
    for $\ngen \in \{3, 4\}$, with $\ngen = 3$ inside.

  \item \textbf{SU Pati--Salam} ($\SU{4}_{\mathrm{PS}} \supset \SU{3}_c$
    as magnetic): electric $\SU{2\ngen - 4}$, bound $\ngen \geq 3$,
    window-compatible for $\ngen \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$, with $\ngen = 3$ on
    the upper edge.

  \item \textbf{Sp duality} ($\SU{2}_L \cong \Sp{1}$ as magnetic):
    electric $\Sp{2\ngen - 3}$, bound $\ngen \geq 2$, window-compatible
    for $\ngen \in \{2, 3\}$.  The upper bound $\ngen \leq 3$ is the
    assumption $b_0^{\mathrm{mag}}(\SU{2}_L) \geq 0$ with supersymmetric
    coefficients.

  \item \textbf{SO duality} ($\SO{6} \cong \SU{4}$ as magnetic):
    electric $\SO{2\ngen - 2}$, bound $\ngen \geq 3$, window-compatible
    for $\ngen \in \{3, 4, 5, 6\}$, with $\ngen = 3$ on the upper edge.

  \item \textbf{Kutasov--Schwimmer} ($k \geq 2$): window
    $2\Nc/(2k-1) \leq \Nf \leq 2\Nc$, giving $\ngen \leq 3$ for every $k$
    with $\ngen = 3$ on the free-magnetic edge; $k = 1$ reduces to SU
    Seiberg duality.
\end{enumerate}

Under this convention, $\ngen = 3$ is the unique value at which both the
SU and the Sp frame sit inside or on the SUSY conformal window:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ngen = 3}\,.
\end{equation}
Every route in Table~\ref{tab:master} contains $\ngen = 3$.  The
selection rests on the assumptions of Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}: with
strict inequalities the SU and Sp ranges do not overlap, and with the
non-supersymmetric Standard Model coefficient the Sp upper bound relaxes
to $\ngen \leq 5$.

If the conjectured non-supersymmetric extension of Seiberg duality holds,
and both the QCD and the electroweak sectors admit dual descriptions that
satisfy these assumptions, the requirement that both frames sit inside or
on the conformal window fixes the number of generations at three.
