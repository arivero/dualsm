# A low-degree symmetric potential cannot isolate an interior phase

Section archived on 20 November 2026 from *Explaining alphaology*. [Original chapter](https://explaining-alphaology.al-rivero.chatgpt.site/geometry-duality/#phase-potential)

Let a Hermitian family field X have positive eigenvalues $x_i$ of mass dimension one. Suppose another part of the potential fixes its scale and imposes a Koide cone on the quadratic mass operator:

$$
e_1=\sum x_i=3s,\qquad e_2=\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j=\frac32s^2.
$$

Any permutation-invariant polynomial in three eigenvalues is a polynomial in $e_1,e_2,e_3$, with degrees one, two and three respectively. At total degree at most five, its dependence on $e_3$ is at most linear. After fixing $e_1,e_2$,

$$
V\big|_{\rm cone}=C+D e_3=C^{\prime}+D^{\prime}\cos3\delta.
$$

Thus $\partial_\delta V=-3D^{\prime}\sin3\delta$. For an interior positive nondegenerate shape, $0<\delta<\pi/12$, this vanishes only if $D^{\prime}=0$, in which case the phase is flat.

Under these assumptions, a renormalizable polynomial of the eigenvalues alone cannot select an isolated interior phase on an exact fixed-scale cone. Extra fields, non-polynomial quantum terms, non-symmetric tensors, or higher-degree operators evade the statement. It does not exclude potentials with such additional structure.
