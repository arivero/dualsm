# Exercise 02 Assessment
Source: `exercises/exercise-02.tex`

Scope: This sheet is a solid introduction to the conformal window, with a clean progression from one-loop asymptotic freedom to Banks-Zaks fixed points and then to the exact SUSY conformal window. It is conceptually broader than Exercise 1, but the structure remains mostly cumulative.

Strengths: Problem 1 is an effective warm-up because it turns the beta-function formula into numerical intuition across several flavor counts. Problem 2 is the strongest pedagogical unit: it introduces the two-loop fixed point, asks for physical conditions on the zero, and then compares two nearby flavor choices so students can see how reliability changes with coupling size. Problem 3 is also well designed as a bridge from perturbative intuition to exact supersymmetric statements. The window table for SU(2), SU(3), and SU(5) is a useful synthesis exercise, and the comparison with the non-SUSY case makes the motivation for later duality work explicit.

Concerns: The main issue is that several explanations are more interpretive than derivational. In Problem 1, the “factor of 4” explanation for the Dirac contribution is too hand-wavy and risks teaching the wrong counting logic. In Problem 2, the reliability estimate for the Banks-Zaks point is asserted in qualitative terms without much justification; the phrase “marginally reliable” is reasonable, but the threshold for that judgment is not made explicit. The biggest pedagogical gap is in Problem 3, where the lower boundary is derived from the meson unitarity bound. That argument is standard, but the sheet presents it as if it were a general proof of confinement below 3Nc/2, which overstates what has been established. The final comparison section also mixes exact SUSY results with much less settled non-SUSY estimates; that contrast is useful, but it should be made more explicit that the non-SUSY lower boundary is not known rigorously.

Suggestions: Tighten the explanation of beta-function coefficients so students see exactly what is counted per Weyl or Dirac fermion. In the Banks-Zaks problem, state the criterion for “perturbatively reliable” in terms of the size of the fixed-point coupling rather than in prose alone. In the conformal-window problem, add one sentence distinguishing the exact upper boundary from the inferred lower boundary mechanism, so students do not leave with the impression that both are equally rigorous in the non-SUSY case.
