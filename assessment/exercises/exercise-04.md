# Exercise 04 Assessment
Source: `exercises/exercise-04.tex`

Scope: This sheet is a mixed-geometry synthesis exercise spanning N=2 decomposition, Fayet-Iliopoulos D-terms, APS deformation, self-duality, and a short bridge back to the conformal window. The topic sequence is ambitious and relevant, but the internal pacing is uneven.

Strengths: Problem 1 is a strong and coherent introduction to N=2-to-N=1 decomposition. It cleanly separates the vector multiplet, adjoint chiral, and hypermultiplet pieces, and it uses the beta-function check to tie field content to dynamics. Problem 2 is also well constructed: the FI-term vacuum analysis is concrete, the dimensional analysis is explicit, and the transition from D-flatness to symmetry breaking is easy to follow. The challenge-style later parts are good for showing how the N=2 story connects to monopole condensation and confinement.

Concerns: The biggest weakness is that the sheet does not maintain a consistent level of rigor across the problems. Problem 1’s discussion of the N=2 R-symmetry versus the N=1 R-symmetry contains a conceptual slip that could mislead students: the text asserts that the two symmetries “coincide” only at a point where the numerical values still do not agree. That should be corrected, because it undercuts the reliability of an otherwise solid setup. In Problem 3, the F-term elimination of `\Phi` is fine algebraically, but the later self-duality discussion is too compressed and risks making the duality look tautological rather than nontrivial. Problem 4 is the most ambitious, but it mixes exact statements, heuristic analogies, and unsettled physics in a way that is hard to separate. The final section on the conformal window is useful, yet it is presented with stronger confidence than the underlying non-SUSY evidence supports.

Suggestions: Fix the R-symmetry comparison in Problem 1 so the N=2 and N=1 conventions are clearly distinguished. In Problem 3, add a short explanatory paragraph on why integrating out the adjoint leaves the quartic or mesonic effective description expected from Seiberg duality. For Problem 4, explicitly label which statements are exact, which are standard heuristics, and which are interpretive analogies. That would preserve the strong physical narrative while reducing the chance that students treat the less certain claims as established results.
