# Exercise 03 Assessment
Source: `exercises/exercise-03.tex`

Scope: This is the most technically dense sheet in the set. It aims to move students from anomaly matching for SQCD into explicit Seiberg-duality checks, mass deformations, and R-charge bookkeeping. The overall arc is good, but the level jumps sharply between parts.

Strengths: The first problem is a strong capstone for anomaly matching because it asks for a full electric/magnetic comparison and makes students tabulate the quantum numbers before computing anything. That is good pedagogy: it forces the structure to be understood before the arithmetic. The mass-deformation problem is also effective because it connects a formal duality statement to a concrete Higgsing mechanism on the magnetic side. Problem 3 is the right kind of synthesis for this stage: derive the non-anomalous R-charge, map it through duality, and then connect it back to the conformal window and the superpotential.

Concerns: The sheet sometimes assumes too much familiarity with duality conventions. In Problem 1, the anomaly list is substantial, but the solution compresses several representation-theory steps into multiplicity factors without enough explanation of why each field contributes with the chosen sign. That is manageable for advanced students, but it makes the exercise less diagnostic of understanding. In Problem 2, the F-term-to-Higgsing argument is correct in spirit, but it jumps from a meson VEV to a gauge-group reduction without clearly identifying the gauge orientation or the eaten degrees of freedom. Students may reproduce the algebra while missing the mechanism. In Problem 3, the derivation of the magnetic R-charges is slightly under-scaffolded: the sheet states the result cleanly, but the route from anomaly cancellation plus superpotential charge 2 is not fully unpacked. That is a missed teaching opportunity because this is one of the most reusable techniques in later material.

Suggestions: Add one short preamble before Problem 1 that reminds students how to convert a left-handed antifundamental into a cubic anomaly sign. In Problem 2, include one sentence explicitly identifying which vacuum expectation value breaks SU(3) to SU(2) and why the rank drops by one. In Problem 3, separate the derivation of `R[M]`, `R[q]`, and `R[Q]` into explicit substeps so the logic is visible rather than inferred. That would make the sheet more usable as a reference for later exercises.
