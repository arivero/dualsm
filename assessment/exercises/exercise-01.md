# Exercise 01 Assessment
Source: `exercises/exercise-01.tex`

Scope: This sheet is a good bridge from anomaly-cancellation warm-up to full 't Hooft anomaly matching. It moves from Standard Model gauge anomalies to generic SU(N) anomaly counting and then to chiral matter, so the conceptual progression is coherent and mostly well paced.

Strengths: The opening problem is concrete and well scaffolded: it explicitly tells students to convert right-handed fields to left-handed conjugates, and the worked solution models the bookkeeping carefully. The general-anomaly problem is also pedagogically strong because it extracts the invariant pattern from the special case and then states clearly which anomalies must be matched and why U(1)_A is excluded. The final chiral SU(5)-type example is useful because it connects anomaly cancellation to the Standard Model and to later dualized-SM material.

Concerns: The main weakness is that the second problem's solution is too self-correcting in a way that may confuse students. It starts by proposing nucleons as the IR matching states, then walks into a mismatch, then resolves the mismatch by changing the question being matched. That sequence is pedagogically risky because it blurs the difference between a false candidate, a true anomaly constraint, and the special role of SU(2) global anomalies. A student could come away thinking the original UV/IR anomaly tables were wrong rather than that the problem statement needed a narrower target. More generally, the worked solutions sometimes carry the burden of defining the exact anomaly conventions instead of leaving students room to derive them. In the SU(5) chiral example, the discussion of global U(1) charges is useful, but the argument about “no gauge-invariant mass term” would benefit from a sharper distinction between bilinears, Yukawa couplings, and gauge-invariant composite operators.

Suggestions: Rework Problem 2 so the target anomalies are stated more cleanly up front, and avoid the false-start nucleon derivation unless it is explicitly framed as a cautionary aside. Consider adding one intermediate prompt in Problem 3 that asks students to justify multiplicity factors before summing, since that is where many first-time errors happen. The sheet would also benefit from a brief note distinguishing anomaly cancellation from anomaly matching right before the first solution, so students do not conflate the two.
