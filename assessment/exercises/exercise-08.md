# Exercise 8 Assessment
Source: `exercises/exercise-08.tex`
Scope: content quality of the meson-identification exercises, with emphasis on quantum numbers, chiral symmetry, and the hierarchy discussion.

## Strengths
This is the strongest pedagogical sequence in the batch. Problem 1 starts from explicit flavour content and charge assignments, Problem 2 moves to the scalar/pseudoscalar distinction and the GMOR relation, and Problem 3 connects the meson discussion back to the broader VEV-hierarchy story. That ordering gives students a clear path from concrete bookkeeping to deeper symmetry breaking ideas. The use of standard QCD inputs also grounds the exercise well.

## Concerns
The main risk is over-claiming on the duality side. Several parts of the set are standard QCD, but the wording sometimes slides into treating the dual meson interpretation as more concrete than it really is. In particular, the solution to Problem 2 identifies the scalar fluctuation $\sigma_{12}$ with the $a_0(980)^+$ meson, which is a model-dependent identification that should be presented much more cautiously. Likewise, the statement that "the pion is the dual meson" is correctly flagged as misleading, but the prompt could go further in separating the standard QCD facts from the conjectured duality map.

Problem 1(d) is also a little uneven. Asking for the $D$-meson family is a reasonable extension, but the solution then drifts into an isospin aside that is not really needed for the task. That can distract students from the intended lesson, which is the direct reading of quark content and charge from the flavour matrix.

In Problem 3, the hierarchy question is pedagogically useful but slightly under-specified. The solution effectively says that one "single" geometric suppression works only after absorbing wide variations into $O(1)$ coefficients. That is probably the right physics attitude, but the prompt should say so more explicitly, otherwise students may think they are expected to extract a unique $\epsilon$.

## Suggestions
Keep the standard QCD material and the conjectural duality interpretation visually and verbally distinct. The current text already uses the "if the conjectured duality holds" qualifier, but the exercises would be clearer if the model-dependent meson identifications were marked as such in the prompts, not only in the solutions.

For Problem 3, rephrase the hierarchy fit as an approximate parametric test rather than a strict single-parameter reconstruction. That would better match the intended lesson and reduce confusion about how much freedom is allowed in the $O(1)$ coefficients.

Overall, the content is solid and the sequencing is strong; the main improvement needed is tighter language around what is standard spectroscopy versus what is duality-dependent interpretation.
