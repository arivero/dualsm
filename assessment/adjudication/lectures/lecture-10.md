## Why Keep/Revise/Replace
The first-pass assessment gets the lecture's central problem exactly right: the source is built around the mismatch between the scalar lowest component of the SUSY meson superfield and the pseudoscalar status of the familiar light mesons. Its praise for the chiral-symmetry-breaking and linear-sigma-model resolution is justified, as is its recognition of the GMOR calculation as an anchor in ordinary QCD. The concern about the strongest DSM bridge claim is also fair: the lecture's statement that the Higgs matrix and the meson matrix are "different presentations of the same underlying object" is rhetorically stronger than the rest of the file.

Revision is still needed because the first pass under-credits how many safeguards the source already supplies. The convention box distinguishes standard QCD meson identification from the conjectural duality interpretation. The `6 x 6` table caption and the dedicated top-quark warning box limit the formal heavy sector. The ChPT section explicitly confines itself to light mesons and hands heavy sectors to HQET or NRQCD. The GMOR remark states plainly that the pion mass comes from standard QCD inputs, not from duality. The late "Honest framing" box again says the payoff is conceptual and structural, not predictive spectroscopy.

The first pass also misses two major bridge mechanisms that are not decorative add-ons: the Sannino--Schechter auxiliary-field story and Komargodski's hidden-local-symmetry interpretation. Those sections are part of what makes the lecture more than a meson naming table plus a DSM analogy.

## Minimum Changes Needed
- Keep the criticism of the strongest Higgs-meson identity claim, but localize it to that specific rhetoric.
- Add that the lecture already differentiates formal matrix entries, physical light mesons, and conjectural DSM interpretation in multiple places.
- Mention the Sannino--Schechter and Komargodski bridges as major strengths.
- Recast any broad concern about over-identifying the matrix with the hadron spectrum as already largely managed by the source.
