## Why Keep/Revise/Replace

The praise for the warning about fermion versus superfield `R`-charges is fully supported (`lectures/lecture-03.tex:33-54`, `224-285`), and the complaint that the later anomaly checks become algebra-dense is fair, especially once the text reaches mixed `U(1)` anomalies and `Tr[R^3]` (`lectures/lecture-03.tex:457-608`).

Still, the first-pass assessment cannot stand unchanged because two of its concrete recommendations are contradicted by the source. It proposes adding a compact summary table of electric and magnetic representations before the anomaly section, but the lecture already has precisely those tables immediately before the anomaly analysis (`lectures/lecture-03.tex:229-278`). It also asks for more prominent "status as a conjecture" language, even though the lecture already includes a boxed section titled "Seiberg Duality: Status as a Conjecture" that explicitly says the duality is not mathematically proved and lists the evidentiary checks (`lectures/lecture-03.tex:765-796`). Those are not minor omissions; they show that parts of the first pass were not sufficiently anchored to the actual document.

What remains useful is the concern about the mass-deformation argument. The Higgsing step from `SU(N_f-N_c)` to `SU(N_f-N_c-1)` is stated briskly, and the scale-matching paragraph openly sends the full argument back to Seiberg's paper (`lectures/lecture-03.tex:636-710`). The assessment also under-credits the source's concrete support for the `Tr[R^3]` identity, which is delegated to a companion verification script rather than merely asserted (`lectures/lecture-03.tex:567-608`).

## Minimum Changes Needed

Remove the suggestion for a representation summary table, since the source already supplies it. Soften the conjecture-status criticism and, if desired, rephrase "proof strategy" as "evidence strategy" to match the lecture's own boxed caution.
