## Why Keep/Revise/Replace
The first-pass assessment gets the lecture's main architecture right. The source does move from the general electric/magnetic ansatz to the Pati-Salam embedding, then to the specialized `N = 3`, `N_f = 6` spectrum, and finally to a long anomaly-cancellation check. Its praise for the hypercharge verification table is also fully deserved, and the concern that the `n_g >= 3` result is model-dependent rather than a general Standard Model theorem is exactly right.

Revision is required because one of the assessment's main criticisms misdescribes the source. The first pass says the lecture does not sharply enough distinguish the QCD-sector duality from the fuller Pati-Salam extension, especially in its treatment of leptons. But the lecture directly anticipates that issue through a dedicated warning box, "Leptons in the QCD dual vs. Pati--Salam," and explicitly states which framework it is following. So the conceptual fork is real, but it is not left implicit.

The first pass also underplays a major structural feature of the lecture: it is not just model-building plus field tables. A very large fraction of the source is devoted to six anomaly conditions for `\SU{3}_c \times \SU{2}_L \times \UU{1}_Y`, followed by an explicit reminder that anomaly matching is necessary but not sufficient. That bookkeeping is one of the lecture's central pillars and should have been treated as such.

## Minimum Changes Needed
- Replace the claim that the QCD-versus-Pati-Salam fork is insufficiently signposted with a narrower point: the lecture still moves quickly among `\SU{3}_c`, `\SU{4}_{PS}`, and `\SU{2}_{el}` and may tax readers' tracking.
- Add the anomaly-matching section as a principal strength, not a peripheral check.
- Keep the caution about compositeness claims, but frame it as pressure on interpretation rather than evidence of missing conditional language throughout.

## Notes For Final Summary
Good read on the lecture's ambition and risks, but it misses an explicit warning box already handling its biggest concern and under-credits the anomaly work.
