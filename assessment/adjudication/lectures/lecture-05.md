## Why Keep/Revise/Replace
The first-pass assessment is directionally strong, but it overstates a few deficits that the lecture already addresses explicitly. It correctly identifies the lecture's main arc: chiral gauge dynamics, MAC as heuristic channel-selection, the `\SU{5}` tumbling example, complementarity, and then a bridge toward non-SUSY duality. Its praise for the dedicated MAC caution is well grounded, as is its emphasis on the pedagogical value of the `\SU{5}` worked example and the complementarity section.

The problem is that several of the proposed fixes are already in the source. The lecture already contains a substantial "what this does and does not prove" discipline through the MAC warning box, the anomaly-matching remark after the `\UU{1}_B^3` mismatch, the Lecture 6 preview, and the final comparison table separating tumbling, complementarity, and duality. The first pass therefore understates how carefully the text already partitions heuristic dynamics, exact anomaly constraints, and conjectural duality language.

Its anomaly-matching concern also needs narrowing. The `-125` versus `-131` mismatch is not presented as an accidental loose end. The source uses it deliberately to show that MAC identifies a preferred condensation channel while anomaly matching still constrains the surviving massless spectrum. That is a stronger and more intentional pedagogical move than the first pass allows.

## Minimum Changes Needed
Revise, not replace. Keep the overall positive judgment, but:

- Acknowledge that the lecture already supplies a dependency map in the Section 5 narrative and Section 6 comparison table.
- Recast the anomaly point as an intentional demonstration of MAC's insufficiency, not a hand-wave.
- Soften the suggestion that the lecture needs a separate "what this proves" paragraph, because that function is already distributed through boxes and summary framing.

## Notes For Final Summary
Useful first pass with a real interpretive handle on the lecture, but it repeatedly recommends additions the source already contains in structured form.
