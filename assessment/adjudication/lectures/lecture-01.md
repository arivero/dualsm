## Why Keep/Revise/Replace
The first-pass assessment is directionally sound, but it overstates two weaknesses and asks for one addition the lecture already provides. Its strengths section tracks the source well: Lecture 1 is indeed organized around the distinction between gauge-anomaly cancellation and 't Hooft anomaly matching (`lectures/lecture-01.tex:179-298`), and the QCD warm-up correctly uses the failed baryon-only IR account plus the WZW repair to show why anomaly matching is physically constraining rather than ceremonial (`lectures/lecture-01.tex:490-529`). It is also fair that the opening course goals are broader than what this lecture itself proves (`lectures/lecture-01.tex:40-50`).

What does not fully hold up is the claim that the WZW role needs to be "made more explicit" as though it were largely absent. The source already includes a dedicated resolution paragraph, an explicit matched anomaly equation with separate baryon and WZW contributions, and a follow-up remark stating that the WZW coefficient is fixed by anomaly matching (`lectures/lecture-01.tex:503-529`). That can still feel brisk to a first-time reader, but it is not an omitted bridge. The conformal-window criticism is similarly too strong. The source already marks the non-SUSY lower boundary as debated in prose and in a warning box, then labels the SUSY window as a preview and exact (`lectures/lecture-01.tex:801-843`). Finally, the requested closing bridge is already present in the summary and Lecture 2 preview (`lectures/lecture-01.tex:975-1013`).

So the assessment should stand only after targeted correction: keep its core praise and the course-goals concern, but narrow the rest.

Acknowledge that the exact-versus-heuristic conformal-window distinction is already clearly signposted. Remove the suggestion asking for a closing paragraph that the lecture already has.
