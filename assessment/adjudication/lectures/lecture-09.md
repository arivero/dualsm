## Why Keep/Revise/Replace
The first-pass assessment is solid enough to stand. It correctly identifies the lecture as a broad survey of the SUSY duality landscape and accurately highlights the strongest elements: the range from Kutasov--Schwimmer through SO, Sp, s-confinement, product-group dualities, and the cascade; the value of explicit anomaly-matching calculations for the non-SU cases; and the utility of the synthesis table that folds the survey back toward the dualised Standard Model.

The audit is right that the source has a somewhat stronger through-line than the first pass emphasizes. From the opening recap onward, the lecture is explicitly asking whether Seiberg duality is special to `\SU{N}` with fundamentals and then answering that question by successive generalizations. It is also true that the source already signals Kutasov--Schwimmer as the directly relevant family for the DSM and uses SO/Sp as evidence of structural breadth rather than direct model input.

Even so, those points do not make the first pass materially unreliable. Saying the lecture can sometimes feel like a well-curated list is a fair reader-facing judgment for a document this broad, especially once it reaches product groups and the cascade. Likewise, the desire for sharper "why this family matters" signposting is not contradicted by the fact that some of that signposting already exists. The first pass remains source-grounded and useful as a final assessment, even if the audit sharpens its emphasis.
