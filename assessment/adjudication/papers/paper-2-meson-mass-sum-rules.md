## Why Keep/Revise/Replace
The first-pass assessment is largely sound. It correctly sees that the paper is strongest when it distinguishes standard QCD content from the one framework-dependent scalar-democracy hierarchy test. That reading matches the manuscript's own later accounting: the numerical summary and discussion classify Sum Rules I-IV and VI as standard ChPT/HQET/additive-quark-model material, while only Sum Rule V is presented as genuinely DSM-specific (`paper.tex` lines 772-792, 800-816, 892-904). The first pass is also right that the paper's title and abstract overstate the novelty, that several numerical checks depend on scheme-sensitive or fitted inputs, and that the scalar-democracy relations behave more like consistency checks than sharp forecasts because the `O(1)` coefficients remain free (`paper.tex` lines 819-831).

That said, a KEEP ruling would be too generous because the first pass misses a direct internal inconsistency that materially affects the novelty claim. The abstract states that sum rules "V-VI encode the additional structure of the scalar democracy framework and provide non-trivial consistency checks" (`paper.tex` lines 75-79). Later sections reverse that classification: the table labels VI as standard QCD (`paper.tex` lines 766-767), the numerical assessment says I-IV and VI are standard-QCD consequences (`paper.tex` lines 774-780), the discussion says only V is genuinely new (`paper.tex` lines 802-805), and the conclusion/epistemic note repeat that only V is framework-dependent (`paper.tex` lines 883-904). That is not a minor wording issue; it is a contradiction in the paper's statement of what is actually new.

The first-pass assessment also slightly inherits the source's early overstatement by speaking of a "handful" of model-dependent relations. On the paper's own final accounting, there is really one model-dependent family here: the scalar-democracy hierarchy relations grouped as Sum Rule V. Everything else is best described as standard QCD material reorganized within the meson-matrix language.

Because the first pass already gets the paper's main strengths and weaknesses right, replacement would be excessive. But revision is required so the final assessment reflects the paper's most important internal novelty inconsistency rather than merely its general framing problem.

## Minimum Changes Needed
1. Explicitly flag the abstract-versus-body contradiction over whether Sum Rule VI is DSM-specific or standard HQET/QCD.
2. Tighten "new sum rules from the matrix" language to "known sum rules reorganized by the matrix, plus one model-dependent hierarchy family."
3. Replace "handful of model-dependent relations" with the narrower source-grounded statement that only Sum Rule V is genuinely framework-specific on the paper's own later account.
4. Keep the existing cautions about scheme sensitivity, fitted inputs, and free `O(1)` coefficients.
