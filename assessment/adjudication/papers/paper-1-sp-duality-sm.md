## Why Keep/Revise/Replace
The first-pass assessment is directionally correct and should not be discarded, but it is not strong enough to stand unchanged. It correctly identifies the paper's real center of gravity: despite proposal-style framing, the manuscript ultimately lands on a negative structural result, namely that the minimal Sp construction does not furnish Higgs doublets. That reading is well supported by Section 5, especially the claim that the antisymmetric product of two `SU(2)_L` doublets is a singlet (`paper.tex` lines 617-645), and by the conclusion's explicit "no-go result" language (`paper.tex` lines 969-975). The first pass is also right to flag uneven evidentiary quality in the anomaly discussion and to warn that speculative rescue mechanisms should not be treated as achieved fixes.

What prevents a KEEP ruling is that the assessment understates the severity of the source's internal inconsistencies. The source is not only misframed; it directly contradicts itself on the central meson/Higgs question. The abstract says the antisymmetric meson yields 66 real scalar degrees of freedom that "decompose into Higgs doublets" (`paper.tex` lines 96-102), and the early summary likewise describes Higgs-like multiplets (`paper.tex` lines 173-176). Section 5 then argues the opposite: each meson component is an `SU(2)_L` singlet, not a doublet (`paper.tex` lines 617-645), and the conclusion repeats that no Higgs doublets are produced (`paper.tex` lines 956-974). The first-pass assessment calls this "scope drift," but the stronger and more accurate diagnosis is direct internal contradiction.

It also misses a second presentation-level inconsistency: the abstract counts 66 real scalar degrees of freedom (`paper.tex` line 97), whereas the meson-content section later says there are 66 complex scalar degrees of freedom (`paper.tex` lines 575-580). Given how heavily the paper relies on representation counting, that mismatch matters. Finally, the assessment's praise that the anomaly section provides a "real technical backbone" is somewhat too generous, since the manuscript fully verifies only the mixed `SU(2N_f)^2 U(1)_R` anomaly and then stumbles on the cubic anomaly before deferring to citation (`paper.tex` lines 477-555).

## Minimum Changes Needed
1. Upgrade the diagnosis from framing mismatch to an explicit abstract/body/conclusion contradiction on whether the Sp meson yields Higgs doublets.
2. Note the 66-real versus 66-complex counting inconsistency.
3. Temper the anomaly praise to reflect that one mixed anomaly is worked out explicitly, while the cubic anomaly discussion is unresolved on the page.
4. Keep the existing point that the paper reads better as a structural no-go analysis than as an affirmative UV-completion proposal.

## Notes For Final Summary
Useful first pass, but not final-ready. The decisive issue is omission of internal contradictions, not misunderstanding of the paper's overall direction. Final summary should present this source as a structurally interesting but self-inconsistent no-go-style analysis whose headline claims need cleanup before they can be trusted.
