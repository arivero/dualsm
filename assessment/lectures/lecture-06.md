# Lecture 06 Assessment

## Source
`lectures/lecture-06.tex`

## Scope
This lecture is the epistemic hinge of the course. It explains what survives when supersymmetry is removed, surveys the main non-SUSY duality proposals, and repeatedly distinguishes exact constraints from conjectural interpretations.

## Strengths
The lecture is unusually careful about epistemic status. It does a good job of separating proven SUSY duality machinery from the much weaker non-SUSY situation, and the repeated insistence that anomaly matching is exact while the duality interpretation is conjectural is pedagogically sound. The historical sequence is also effective: toy model, tension, QCD dual, SM-as-magnetic proposal, emergent SUSY, then the central vulnerability.

The strongest part is the way the lecture names the limitation instead of burying it. Saying that non-SUSY duality has no independent cross-validation beyond anomaly matching gives the reader a clear standard for evaluating the rest of the course. That honesty makes the later DSM material more defensible, not less.

## Concerns
- The lecture compresses several distinct proposals into a single narrative thread. That is efficient, but it can blur the difference between “historical evidence,” “consistency checks,” and “candidate duality.”
- The phrase “only anomaly matching survives” is directionally right, but too absolute if read literally, because the lecture still uses perturbative fixed points, beta-function estimates, and large-$N$ arguments as supporting structure.
- The Cheng--Shadmi tension is important, but the discussion stops at the problem statement. Readers may want a clearer sense of what exactly is unresolved versus what is simply incompatible with the desired limit.

## Suggestions
- Add a compact taxonomy near the top: proven, conjectural, perturbative evidence, and heuristic motivation. The lecture already contains those distinctions; making them explicit would reduce ambiguity.
- Give each proposal one sentence on its status and one sentence on its relevance to the DSM, so the reader can separate history from application.
- In the vulnerability section, state explicitly that later lectures are assuming the conjecture as a working hypothesis rather than advancing new evidence for it. That would help the course remain internally disciplined.
