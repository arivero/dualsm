# Lecture 08 Assessment

## Source
`lectures/lecture-08.tex`

## Scope
This lecture derives the scalar-democracy picture, Planck-suppressed flavour operators, electroweak symmetry breaking in the 18-Higgs sector, and the parametric matching of scales. It is the quantitative core of the DSM narrative.

## Strengths
The lecture is strongest when it keeps the distinction between parametric consistency and prediction explicit. That restraint is important, because the framework makes several ambitious claims but does not actually determine the free coefficients. The universal Yukawa structure is also explained cleanly: the reader can see how the hierarchy is shifted from the Yukawa matrices into the VEV pattern.

The scale-matching section is effective as an order-of-magnitude consistency check. The relation between $\Lambda_D$, $v$, and $M_{\text{Pl}}$ is physically intuitive, and the one-loop estimate for $\Lambda_{\QCD}$ gives the reader something numerically concrete without pretending to be a precision derivation. The collider-signature section is similarly useful because it turns the abstract spectrum into a phenomenological target.

## Concerns
- The lecture sometimes moves from “natural parametric size” to “explains the observed value” too quickly. The distinction is stated in the text, but the rhetoric can still feel stronger than the evidence.
- The discussion of the 18 Higgs doublets and the VEV hierarchy is dense, and the logic of why only one doublet is light could be made more transparent.
- Several numerical conventions, especially the various uses of `v`, `246 GeV`, and `174 GeV`, are easy to confuse. The content is internally workable, but the notation carries more burden than it should.

## Suggestions
- Add a short summary table that separates inputs, outputs, and free parameters. That would help keep the reader oriented through the many scale estimates.
- State explicitly that the Planck-scale operator coefficients are doing most of the explanatory work on the flavour side. This would reduce the risk of over-reading the model as predictive.
- Consider a concise “what is actually derived here” paragraph at the end of the lecture so that the scale arguments do not feel like a chain of successive reinterpretations.
