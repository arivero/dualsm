# Lecture 05 Assessment

## Source
`lectures/lecture-05.tex`

## Scope
This lecture introduces the MAC criterion, tumbling, and Fradkin--Shenker complementarity, then uses them to motivate the later duality programme. It is the first place where the notes move from established non-perturbative physics to a heuristic bridge toward duality.

## Strengths
The lecture has a strong pedagogical arc. It starts with familiar QCD-like intuition, then isolates the MAC criterion as a manageable heuristic, and finally shows how tumbling and complementarity supply a physical story for gauge symmetry breaking and phase continuity. The explicit warning that MAC is not a theorem is valuable and sets a good epistemic tone. The $\SU{5}$ worked example is especially effective because it gives the reader a concrete condensation channel, a symmetry-breaking pattern, and a reason to care about anomaly matching.

The complementarity section is also a strength. It does not merely state the Fradkin--Shenker theorem, but explains the conditions under which it applies and how those conditions fail in other settings. That makes the final bridge to duality more credible than if the lecture had treated “Higgs = confinement” as a slogan.

## Concerns
- The tumbling example is pedagogically useful, but the anomaly-matching discussion is underdeveloped relative to the size of the claim. The text shows a mismatch and then gestures to “additional composite massless fermions,” which risks reading as an unresolved patch rather than a controlled lesson.
- The MAC discussion sometimes sounds more deterministic than it is. The lecture correctly says the criterion is heuristic, but some later language still reads as if the channel selection is almost automatic.
- The transition from tumbling and complementarity to duality is conceptually strong, but it remains an analogy unless the lecture keeps emphasizing where the logic stops.

## Suggestions
- Add a short “what this does and does not prove” paragraph after the $\SU{5}$ example to separate the illustrative role of the computation from the full dynamical claim.
- Make the anomaly-matching subsection more explicit about what is known from the literature versus what is being simplified for pedagogy.
- Consider ending the lecture with a compact dependency map: MAC chooses a channel, tumbling propagates the breaking, complementarity links phases, and anomaly matching constrains the spectrum. That would sharpen the bridge to Lecture 6.
