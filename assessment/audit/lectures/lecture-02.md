# Lecture 2 Audit

## Source Pair
`lectures/lecture-02.tex` and `assessment/lectures/lecture-02.md`

## Verdict
Largely accurate, but mildly overstated and somewhat incomplete. The assessment captures the lecture's pedagogical arc well, yet its main criticisms understate how explicitly the source already labels perturbative versus non-perturbative claims and preview material.

## What The Assessment Gets Right
The assessment correctly describes the lecture's structure. The source really does build from the SUSY algebra to chiral superfields, vector superfields, the full `N=1` Lagrangian, the scalar potential, and then the non-renormalisation and NSVZ machinery. Its praise for the degree-of-freedom counting is well founded: both chiral and vector multiplets get explicit off-shell and on-shell counting, which helps the lecture stay accessible to readers coming from ordinary QFT. It is also right that holomorphy, auxiliary fields, and the SQCD symmetry discussion are used as setup for the duality material. The section deriving the anomaly-free `R`-charge is especially systematic and does real conceptual work rather than merely quoting a standard formula.

## What It Misses Or Overstates
The concern about the non-renormalisation theorem sounding too absolute is weaker than the assessment suggests. The theorem itself says "no perturbative quantum corrections," and the very next sentence allows non-perturbative instanton corrections. The follow-up spurion argument repeats that distinction, and a later remark explicitly says the Kähler potential is not protected. So the text already makes the perturbative/non-perturbative boundary quite clearly.

The NSVZ criticism is only partly fair. The one-loop check is compact, but it is not merely asserted: the numerator is written out, the `2N_f` counting from `Q` and `\widetilde Q` is explained, and the vector-multiplet/chiral-multiplet contributions are unpacked again in prose. A reader could still want more intuition, but the source does not present the coefficient as a bare rule.

The assessment also understates how clearly the lecture labels forward references. The magnetic superpotential appears inside an explicitly framed verification remark and again in the Lecture 3 preview. That is a genuine preview, not an unmarked import of later results.

A more important omission is that the assessment does not notice the most awkward passage in the source: the moduli-space dimension "consistency check" becomes internally muddled before correcting itself.

## Recommended Revisions
Keep the positive evaluation of the lecture's pedagogical sequencing. Recast the concerns more narrowly: the magnetic-theory references are acceptable as previews, and the non-renormalisation theorem is already properly qualified, but the NSVZ section could still benefit from cleaner separation between the exact structural formula and the one-loop bookkeeping check. Add a note that the moduli-space dimension consistency remark is the section most in need of tightening.
