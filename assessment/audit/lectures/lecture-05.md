# Audit: Lecture 05 Assessment

## Source Pair
`lectures/lecture-05.tex` and `assessment/lectures/lecture-05.md`

## Verdict
Accurate but incomplete.

## What The Assessment Gets Right
The assessment captures the lecture's basic arc correctly. The source does move from chiral gauge dynamics, to the MAC criterion, to tumbling, to complementarity, and then to a bridge toward later duality claims. It is also right that the lecture treats MAC as a heuristic rather than a theorem. That caution is not incidental: Section 2 contains a dedicated warning box listing four limitations, including the lack of proof, the use of perturbative reasoning at strong coupling, known counterexamples, and the fact that MAC says nothing about the condensation scale. The assessment is also fair to highlight the SU(5) worked example as the lecture's central pedagogical device. The source really does use that example to combine channel selection, gauge breaking SU(5) -> SU(4), and anomaly-matching constraints in one place.

The concern about the tumbling example's anomaly discussion is also substantially grounded in the text. The lecture explicitly computes a U(1)_B^3 mismatch (-125 in the UV versus -131 in the naive IR decomposition) and then says additional massless composites or other subtleties must appear. That is exactly the sort of place where a reader could feel the analysis is suggestive rather than closed.

## What It Misses Or Overstates
What the assessment misses is how much of its own advice the lecture already implements. The source already contains a fairly explicit "this does and does not prove" discipline: the MAC warning box, the anomaly-matching remark after the mismatch, the preview for Lecture 6, and the comparison table in Section 6 all separate tumbling, complementarity, and duality as logically distinct ideas. The final comparison table is especially important because it already functions as the "dependency map" the assessment recommends.

The assessment also slightly understates how deliberate the anomaly mismatch example is. In the source it is not presented as a loose patch; it is used to show that MAC identifies a condensation channel, while anomaly matching constrains the surviving spectrum. That is a sharper pedagogical point than the assessment gives it credit for.

## Recommended Revisions
Revise the assessment to say that the lecture is strongest not only because it warns that MAC is heuristic, but because it already includes a structured separation between tumbling, complementarity, and duality. Reframe the anomaly-matching concern as "the lecture intentionally leaves the SU(4) spectrum unresolved to illustrate why MAC alone is insufficient," rather than implying the text simply hand-waves past the issue. Finally, remove or soften the suggestion that the lecture needs a compact dependency map, because Section 5 plus the comparison table in Section 6 already provide one.
