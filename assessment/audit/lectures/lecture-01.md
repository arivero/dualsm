# Lecture 1 Audit

## Source Pair
`lectures/lecture-01.tex` and `assessment/lectures/lecture-01.md`

## Verdict
Mostly accurate, but somewhat overstated. The assessment identifies the lecture's real organizing themes, yet two of its main concerns under-credit qualifications that are already present in the source.

## What The Assessment Gets Right
The assessment is right that Lecture 1 is built around a clear conceptual spine. The source explicitly separates gauge-anomaly cancellation from 't Hooft anomaly matching in both the review material and the comparison table, then reuses that distinction in the QCD warm-up and the general `SU(N_c)` anomaly discussion. It is also right that the QCD example is pedagogically important: the lecture first shows that a naive baryon-only IR spectrum gives the wrong `SU(2)_L^3` anomaly, then resolves the mismatch through the Wess-Zumino-Witten term. Likewise, the assessment correctly notices that the conformal-window section distinguishes exact statements from non-exact ones. The upper asymptotic-freedom bound is presented as exact, the lower non-SUSY edge is described as debated and method-dependent, and the SUSY conformal window is explicitly boxed as an exact preview result.

## What It Misses Or Overstates
The strongest concern about the QCD warm-up being too brisk is only partly fair. The source already does more than hint at the Goldstone/WZW resolution: it gives a dedicated "Resolution" paragraph, an explicit matched anomaly equation with a WZW contribution, and a follow-up remark stating that the WZW coefficient is fixed by anomaly matching. That means the issue is not absence of explanation so much as whether the explanation is brief.

The conformal-window criticism is also a bit strong. The lecture already marks the non-SUSY lower boundary as approximate in both the prose and a warning box, then labels the SUSY result as a preview and exact. The boundary between heuristic and rigorous material is therefore sharper than the assessment implies.

The assessment also misses that the lecture already contains the kind of closing bridge it asks for: the summary section explicitly states what has been established and previews how those tools feed into Lecture 2 and later Seiberg-duality checks.

## Recommended Revisions
Revise the assessment toward "accurate but slightly overstated." Keep the praise for the anomaly-matching structure and the QCD warm-up. Narrow the concerns to two precise points: first, the course-level goals overpromise relative to what Lecture 1 itself proves; second, the WZW discussion is present and correct but could be slowed down for readers seeing anomaly matching through spontaneous symmetry breaking for the first time. The conformal-window note should acknowledge that the lecture already labels exact versus approximate boundaries clearly.
