# Audit: Lecture 10 Assessment

## Source Pair
`lectures/lecture-10.tex` and `assessment/lectures/lecture-10.md`

## Verdict
Accurate but incomplete, with one fair concern about overstatement.

## What The Assessment Gets Right
The assessment gets the lecture's central problem exactly right: the source is organized around the mismatch between the scalar lowest component of the SUSY meson superfield and the pseudoscalar nature of the familiar light mesons. The lecture really does resolve that by introducing chiral symmetry breaking, the linear sigma model decomposition phi = (v + sigma + i pi)/sqrt(2), and then connecting the phase modes to the pseudoscalar spectrum. It is also right that the GMOR calculation is used as a concrete anchor in ordinary QCD, and right that the lecture distinguishes light-light, heavy-light, heavy-heavy, and top-containing mesons rather than pretending one effective description covers all of them.

The assessment is also justified in questioning the source's statement that the Higgs matrix from Lecture 7 and the meson matrix here are "different presentations of the same underlying object." The source really does make that strong claim in the DSM bridge section, and that is the place where its rhetoric is bolder than the rest of the lecture.

## What It Misses Or Overstates
What the assessment misses is how cautious the source already is on almost every other front. The convention box states that the meson tables are standard QCD while the duality interpretation is conjectural. The 6 x 6 table caption says the top row and column are formal only. Section 2 contains a dedicated top-quark caveat explaining why no top mesons are physical. The ChPT section explicitly restricts itself to the light sector and hands heavy mesons to HQET or NRQCD. The GMOR remark says plainly that the numerical pion mass comes from QCD, not from the duality. The "What the duality adds" preview and the "Honest framing" warning box both insist that the payoff is conceptual and structural, not a new spectroscopy prediction.

The assessment also overlooks two important bridge mechanisms the source spends real effort on: the Sannino-Schechter auxiliary-field story and Komargodski's hidden-local-symmetry interpretation.

## Recommended Revisions
Keep the core praise for the scalar-pseudoscalar resolution and the criticism of the strongest Higgs-meson identity claim. But revise the rest to acknowledge that the source already contains repeated safeguards against over-identifying the formal meson matrix with the full physical hadron spectrum. Add that the lecture's real novelty is not the 6 x 6 table by itself, but the layered bridge: standard QCD classification, linear-sigma reinterpretation, SUSY-to-QCD toy mechanism, hidden local symmetry, and finally the constrained DSM analogy.
