# Audit: Lecture 06 Assessment

## Source Pair
`lectures/lecture-06.tex` and `assessment/lectures/lecture-06.md`

## Verdict
Accurate but incomplete.

## What The Assessment Gets Right
The assessment correctly identifies Lecture 6 as the course's epistemic hinge. The source is unusually explicit about status distinctions: before the document body, in the convention box, throughout the proposal sections, in the central vulnerability section, and again in the preview for Lectures 7-8, the lecture insists that anomaly equations are exact while the duality interpretation is conjectural. The assessment is also right that the lecture's strongest feature is its refusal to hide the main weakness. Section 6 states, in bold, that non-SUSY duality has no independent cross-validation beyond anomaly matching, and the summary repeats the point. That is indeed the lecture's most important intellectual content.

The assessment also gets the historical sequence largely right. The text moves from the four SUSY pillars, to what is lost without SUSY, to Sannino-Schechter, to the Cheng-Shadmi tension, to Sannino's non-SUSY QCD dual, to the Standard Model as magnetic dual, to emergent SUSY, and finally to the central vulnerability.

## What It Misses Or Overstates
What it misses is the degree of formalization. The source does not merely "repeatedly insist" on conjectural status; it makes that a non-negotiable lecture convention. That matters because the assessment frames the epistemic care as a rhetorical virtue, while the source treats it almost as a contract.

Its concern that the phrase "only anomaly matching survives" is too absolute is only partly fair. The lecture itself distinguishes between surviving exact pillars and secondary suggestive evidence. Perturbative fixed points, beta-function estimates, and large-N arguments are presented later as supporting clues, not as pillars that survive on the same footing as holomorphy or moduli-space matching. In other words, the source's stronger claim is deliberate and internally consistent.

The suggestion that later lectures should explicitly state they assume the conjecture as a working hypothesis is already implemented. The preview box for Lectures 7-8 says exactly that, and says every later prediction is conditional.

## Recommended Revisions
Keep the positive assessment of the lecture's epistemic discipline, but make clear that this discipline is built into the lecture's stated conventions, not just its tone. Narrow the criticism of "only anomaly matching survives" to note that the phrase is strong but intentional because the lecture reserves "survives" for exact non-perturbative tools. Add that the Cheng-Shadmi discussion is more concrete than the assessment implies: the unresolved issue is specifically tachyonic magnetic scalars and the resulting wrong symmetry-breaking pattern in the large SUSY-breaking regime.
