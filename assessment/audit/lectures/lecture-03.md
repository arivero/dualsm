# Lecture 3 Audit

## Source Pair
`lectures/lecture-03.tex` and `assessment/lectures/lecture-03.md`

## Verdict
Partly accurate, but with two clear mistakes. The assessment correctly identifies the lecture's proof-by-checks structure and its density, but it claims the source lacks elements that are in fact already present and prominently placed.

## What The Assessment Gets Right
The overall description of the lecture is strong. The source is explicitly organized as a sequence of evidence: phase structure, statement of the electric and magnetic theories, quantum numbers, anomaly matching, mass deformation, and moduli-space consistency. The assessment is also right that the warning about using fermion rather than superfield `R`-charges is valuable; the lecture foregrounds that point in the conventions, in the magnetic-field table, and again in a dedicated warning box before the anomaly calculations. Its concern about the density of the anomaly computations is fair as well. Once the lecture reaches the later coefficients, especially the mixed `U(1)` and `Tr[R^3]` checks, the exposition becomes algebra-heavy and assumes the reader will track multiplicities carefully.

## What It Misses Or Overstates
The assessment mistakenly suggests adding a compact summary table of electric and magnetic representations before the anomaly section. The source already does exactly that: it provides an electric field-content table and a magnetic field-content table immediately before the anomaly-matching section. That recommendation therefore does not reflect the actual text.

Its "status as a conjecture" concern is also overstated. The lecture contains a boxed section titled "Seiberg Duality: Status as a Conjecture" which explicitly says the duality is not mathematically proved and then lists the evidentiary checks. That caution is already more prominent than the assessment acknowledges.

The mass-deformation criticism is more justified. The Higgsing step from `SU(N_f-N_c)` to `SU(N_f-N_c-1)` is stated briskly, and the scale-matching discussion explicitly refers the full argument to Seiberg's paper. A reader new to the subject could reasonably want one more paragraph of physical explanation there.

The assessment also misses a useful feature of the source: the `Tr[R^3]` identity is not merely waved through but delegated to a companion verification script, which is a concrete form of support even if the intermediate algebra is omitted in the text.

## Recommended Revisions
Keep the density and mass-deformation concerns, but remove the claim that the lecture needs a representation summary table and soften the complaint about conjecture-status signaling. A better revision would say that the lecture already contains strong epistemic caution, yet the anomaly section could use more verbal framing between coefficients and the Higgsing argument could use one additional explanatory bridge before the scale-matching paragraph.
