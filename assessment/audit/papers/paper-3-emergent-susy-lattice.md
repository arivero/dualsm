# Title
Audit of First-Pass Assessment for "Lattice Signatures of Emergent Supersymmetry at the Conformal Window Boundary"

## Source Pair
Source paper: `papers/paper-3-emergent-susy-lattice/paper.tex`

Assessment reviewed: `assessment/papers/paper-3-emergent-susy-lattice.md`

## Verdict
Accurate but incomplete.

## What The Assessment Gets Right
The assessment correctly identifies the paper's real center of gravity: it is strongest when it turns the emergent-SUSY conjecture into concrete lattice-facing targets, especially the mass anomalous dimension and the scalar-to-pseudoscalar ratio. That matches the source, which gives explicit numerical `gamma_m = 9/N_f` values for `N_f = 8, 10, 12`, compares them to Banks-Zaks and Schwinger-Dyson expectations, and ties `m_sigma / m_pi` directly to current LatKMI and LSD data.

It is also right that Observables 2, 4, and 5 are materially weaker than 1 and 3. The paper itself concedes that the glueball observable is "not directly available" in the target fundamental-fermion theory and only introduces a proxy. Likewise, the Ward-identity proposal openly says the main challenge is constructing an appropriate supercurrent, especially if one must use composite operators in the electric theory. The flat-direction discussion is indeed imported from SUSY moduli-space reasoning rather than derived from the non-SUSY SU(3) theory under study.

The assessment is also fair that existing `N_f = 8` light-scalar data are suggestive rather than decisive. The source itself admits a dilaton interpretation as a live alternative.

## What It Misses Or Overstates
The main omission is that the paper is more self-limiting than the assessment suggests. The abstract, introduction, Section 3 header, prediction tables, and conclusion repeatedly state that emergent-SUSY claims are conditional and conjectural. So the source does sometimes overreach in tone, but it does not generally hide its epistemic status.

Relatedly, the assessment says the paper overstates discriminating power because the observables are not independent. That criticism has force, but the source already partially acknowledges it: the summary table states that distinguishing emergent SUSY from ordinary conformal dynamics requires at least two observables measured at the same `N_f`. The paper therefore presents five proposed diagnostics, not five fully independent proofs.

One smaller mismatch is the assessment's praise of "volume dependence" as a comparatively coherent strength. In the source, the explicit volume-scaling proposal belongs mainly to the SUSY Ward-identity observable, which is one of the most speculative parts of the manuscript rather than one of the most secure.

## Recommended Revisions
Revise the assessment to keep its core criticism but sharpen the target. It should say that the paper derives conditional predictions under an emergent-SUSY ansatz, then extends those predictions too aggressively for Observables 2, 4, and 5. That is more precise than implying the paper simply forgets its conjectural status.

It should also note that the source already contains substantial caveat language and already treats the `N_f = 8` evidence as intriguing but inconclusive, even if phrases like "strikingly close" and "in tension with" occasionally push the rhetoric too far.

Finally, the assessment should distinguish between the paper's two robust proposal pillars, `gamma_m` and `m_sigma / m_pi`, and its three auxiliary speculative extensions, rather than treating all five proposed observables as equally central.
