# Audit: Dual Meson Mass Sum Rules from the 6x6 Flavour Matrix

## Source Pair
`papers/paper-2-meson-mass-sum-rules/paper.tex` and `assessment/papers/paper-2-meson-mass-sum-rules.md`

## Verdict
Mostly accurate, with one important omission. The assessment captures the paper's real center of gravity very well, especially its novelty-positioning problem and its reliance on scheme-sensitive or fitted inputs, but it does not catch a direct inconsistency in how the source classifies Sum Rule VI.

## What The Assessment Gets Right
The assessment correctly reads the paper as strongest when it distinguishes standard QCD consequences from the scalar-democracy-specific hierarchy test. That is exactly where the source ends up: the numerical summary, discussion, and conclusion all say that Sum Rules I-IV and VI are standard ChPT, HQET, or additive-quark-model results, while only Sum Rule V is genuinely specific to the dualised-SM/scalar-democracy framework. The assessment is also right that the paper itself becomes more honest and precise as it goes on than the title and abstract initially suggest.

Its concerns about evidentiary quality are well grounded in the text. The GMOR check uses a condensate estimate with explicit uncertainty, the HQET comparison uses pole-mass language and then retreats to a scheme caveat, and the scalar-democracy hierarchy test sets `epsilon = m_b/m_t` directly from data before checking the remaining tiers. The assessment's point about free `O(1)` coefficients is also directly supported by the paper's own discussion section, which admits that the framework is explanatory more than sharply predictive unless the coefficient tensor is constrained.

The recommendation to keep the formal `6 x 6` matrix separate from the physical meson spectrum is likewise evidence-based: the paper itself says the top row and column are formal because the top quark does not hadronize, and it treats the physical discussion mostly in a `5 x 5` submatrix with approximate diagonal identifications.

## What It Misses Or Overstates
The major omission is that the source contradicts itself about Sum Rule VI. The abstract says that Sum Rules V-VI encode additional scalar-democracy structure and provide non-trivial checks at the `~10%` level. But the table, assessment section, discussion, conclusion, and epistemic note all later classify VI as standard heavy-quark-symmetry/QCD content rather than a DSM-specific prediction. That internal inconsistency is important because it directly affects the paper's novelty claim.

The assessment also slightly inherits the paper's early overstatement by referring to a "handful" of model-dependent relations. On the paper's own final accounting, there is really one model-dependent family here: Sum Rule V.

## Recommended Revisions
Add an explicit note that the paper is internally inconsistent about whether Sum Rule VI is DSM-specific or standard HQET, and state that the source's own later sections support the narrower claim that only Sum Rule V is genuinely model-dependent. It would also help to sharpen the assessment's language from "new sum rules from the matrix" to "known sum rules reorganized by the matrix, plus one hierarchy-based consistency check." With that addition, the assessment would match the source very closely.
