# Title
Audit of First-Pass Assessment for "Why Three Generations: The n_g >= 3 Bound Across Duality Types"

## Source Pair
Source paper: `papers/paper-4-generation-number/paper.tex`

Assessment reviewed: `assessment/papers/paper-4-generation-number.md`

## Verdict
Incomplete.

## What The Assessment Gets Right
The assessment correctly recognizes that the paper's route-by-route organization is one of its strengths. The SU, Sp, SO, and Kutasov-Schwimmer analyses are each presented in separate sections with explicit electric-group formulas, conformal-window inequalities, and summary tables. It is also right that the paper openly marks the non-SUSY relevance of these dualities as conjectural rather than suppressing that issue.

Its strongest substantive criticism is the treatment of boundary cases, especially the Pati-Salam route at `n_g = 3`. The source explicitly computes `b_0 = 0` there, acknowledges that the theory sits at the upper boundary of asymptotic freedom, and nevertheless retains that case as "still considered viable." The assessment is justified in saying this is a much weaker status than an ordinary interior conformal-window point.

The assessment is also fair that the SO and higher-`k` KS sections should not be read as universal no-go theorems for all possible model building. In the source, both depend on specific gauge-group identifications and flavor-counting choices.

## What It Misses Or Overstates
What the assessment misses is that the paper already contains more internal qualification than the critique credits. The introduction has an "Epistemic convention" paragraph stating that all non-SUSY extrapolations are conjectural. The "Robustness of the `n_g = 3` selection" section explicitly says the upper bound depends on the SUSY Sp conformal window and could relax if the non-SUSY window is wider. The "Model dependence" section separately lists flavor counting and embedding choices as assumptions. Because of that, the claim that the paper should simply be recast as conditional is only partly responsive: much of the body already does this.

The assessment also somewhat overstates the role of SO and KS in the actual core argument. The paper's decisive intersection is SU with Sp; SO is presented as an excluded route and KS with `k >= 2` as a surveyed dead end. Those sections may still be overextended relative to physical motivation, but they are not the load-bearing steps in the final `n_g = 3` derivation.

Finally, the critique implies inconsistency in how boundary cases are counted, but the source is fairly explicit about that choice. The master-table caption says boundary cases are included, and the Pati-Salam `n_g = 3` inclusion is tied to a stated literature convention rather than smuggled in silently.

## Recommended Revisions
Revise the assessment so its main charge is not "the paper fails to state its assumptions," but rather "the paper's title, abstract, and theorem-like phrasing are stronger than its already-qualified body justifies." That better matches the source.

It should focus on two real fragilities: first, the permissive treatment of `b_0 = 0` boundary cases in the Pati-Salam route; second, the dependence of the upper bound `n_g <= 3` on importing the SUSY Sp conformal window into a non-SUSY setting.

It should also distinguish between the paper's actual proof structure, which is SU lower bound plus Sp upper bound, and its broader survey material on SO and KS, which is informative but secondary.
