# Audit: Sp(1) Seiberg Duality as an Alternative UV Completion of the Electroweak Sector

## Source Pair
`papers/paper-1-sp-duality-sm/paper.tex` and `assessment/papers/paper-1-sp-duality-sm.md`

## Verdict
Accurate but incomplete. The assessment correctly identifies the paper's central framing problem and its eventual no-go conclusion, but it understates the severity of the source's internal contradictions and is slightly too generous about the technical solidity of the anomaly section.

## What The Assessment Gets Right
The assessment is right that the paper's strongest substantive result is negative, not constructive. The body of the paper ultimately argues that the minimal Sp construction does not generate Higgs doublets: Section 5 states that the antisymmetric product of two `SU(2)_L` doublets is an `SU(2)_L` singlet, Section 7 treats this as the key difference from the `SU(N)` route, and the conclusion explicitly calls it a "no-go result" for the minimal setup. The assessment is also right that the framing still reads like a UV-completion proposal even though the destination is really a structural obstruction.

It is also fair about the evidentiary chain. The paper does make a clear epistemic distinction between exact SUSY facts and conjectural non-SUSY extrapolation in the introduction and conclusion, and the assessment correctly notices that this caution is not maintained uniformly once conformal-window and phenomenological implications are discussed. Its concern about the cubic-anomaly section is well founded: the text presents a mismatch, attempts a resolution, and then effectively falls back to citation rather than completing an internally self-contained derivation.

## What It Misses Or Overstates
The main omission is that the paper is not merely drifting in scope; it is internally contradictory on a core claim. The abstract says the antisymmetric meson yields 66 real scalar degrees of freedom that "decompose into Higgs doublets," and the early summary likewise says the mesons become Higgs-like multiplets. But Section 5 later argues the opposite: the mesons are `SU(2)_L` singlets, not doublets, and the conclusion repeats that they do not include Higgs doublets. The assessment should have flagged this as a direct contradiction in the source, not just a framing mismatch.

It also misses a second inconsistency in the counting language: the abstract/summary refer to 66 real scalar degrees of freedom, while the meson-content section later says there are 66 complex scalar degrees of freedom. That matters because the paper relies heavily on representation-counting precision.

Finally, "the anomaly-matching section gives the paper a real technical backbone" is somewhat overstated. The paper explicitly verifies one mixed anomaly, but the cubic anomaly discussion is unresolved on the page and deferred to the literature.

## Recommended Revisions
Revise the assessment to say the paper is directionally coherent but internally inconsistent on its central meson/Higgs claim. It should explicitly note the abstract-versus-body contradiction over whether the Sp meson yields Higgs doublets, and the real-versus-complex mismatch in the 66-component counting. It should also temper the praise for the anomaly analysis: the paper demonstrates one explicit anomaly match, not a broadly secure anomaly program. With those changes, the assessment would more accurately reflect the source as a useful structural analysis whose own presentation needs substantial cleanup.
