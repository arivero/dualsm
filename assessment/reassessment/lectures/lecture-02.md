# Lecture 2 XHigh Assessment

Source: `lectures/lecture-02.tex`

Scope: Motivation for supersymmetry; the `N=1` SUSY algebra; chiral and vector superfields; the gauge and matter Lagrangian; the scalar potential; the non-renormalisation theorem; the NSVZ beta function; SQCD field content and symmetries; the anomaly-free `R`-charge; and the classical moduli-space setup.

Strengths: This is a well-built transition lecture. It starts from the algebra and superfield technology, then climbs in a controlled way to the full `N=1` Lagrangian, scalar potential, exact holomorphy statements, and finally SQCD data needed for duality. The degree-of-freedom counting for chiral and vector multiplets is especially useful for readers arriving from ordinary QFT. The source also already contains much of the caution that earlier reviews understated: the non-renormalisation theorem is explicitly limited to perturbative corrections, instanton effects are named as non-perturbative exceptions, the Kahler potential is clearly marked as unprotected, and the magnetic-superpotential remark is visibly framed as a forward reference rather than smuggled in as established material. Tables and bridges are likewise already present: there is a conventions table, an SQCD component-field table, a quantum-number table, a preview box on quantum modifications, and a closing summary/preview that ties directly into Lecture 3.

Concerns: The weakest part of the lecture is the moduli-space dimension discussion. The boxed formula itself may be the intended one, but the surrounding explanation mixes real and complex dimensions, subtracts gauge directions in a confusing way, and the worked `N_f = N_c = 2` “consistency check” becomes internally contradictory before correcting itself. That passage is likely to leave a careful reader less certain than before. A second issue is cross-lecture convention drift. Lecture 1 fixed baryon number with the Seiberg-style normalization `B[Q] = 1/N_c`, while Lecture 2’s field-content table uses `B = +/- 1` after stating that Lecture 1 conventions remain in force. Because later anomaly coefficients depend on this normalization, the shift should be explicit if intentional. A smaller but still real problem is wording in the motivation section: saying SUSY duality can be “proved” and that Lecture 3 will “prove” Seiberg duality overshoots the source’s own later standard, where the duality is treated as a conjecture with strong evidence.

Suggestions: Keep the lecture’s sequence and most of its signposting. The priority is to rewrite the moduli-space-dimension subsection with one consistent counting scheme, explicitly stating whether dimensions are complex or real at each step. The baryon-number normalization should also be fixed by either restoring Lecture 1’s convention or announcing the new normalization when the SQCD table appears. Finally, replace “prove” with language closer to “derive evidence for” or “verify through exact checks,” which would align Lecture 2’s framing with Lecture 3’s more careful epistemic stance without weakening the pedagogy.
