# Lecture 06 XHigh Assessment

## Source
`lectures/lecture-06.tex`

## Scope
This lecture is the course's epistemic hinge. It identifies which pillars of SUSY duality are lost outside supersymmetry, surveys the main non-SUSY extension attempts, isolates the Cheng-Shadmi obstruction, and frames the later dualised Standard Model lectures as conditional applications of a conjecture rather than as established theory.

## Strengths
The best feature of the lecture is that its caution is structural rather than cosmetic. The convention box installs an explicit lecture-wide rule: anomaly equations are exact, but every non-SUSY duality interpretation is conjectural. That rule is reinforced by the "sole surviving tool" warning, the SUSY-versus-non-SUSY comparison table, the repeated "if the duality holds" qualifiers, the preview for Lectures 7-8, and above all the final "central vulnerability" box. The file is not merely careful in tone; it is organized around not letting the reader forget what is and is not known.

The historical survey is also well assembled. Sannino-Schechter is presented as a suggestive toy mechanism rather than a proof, the Cheng-Shadmi discussion names the actual problem instead of invoking vague unease, and the later Sannino proposals are presented as direct conjectures checked by anomaly matching, involution, flavour decoupling, and related consistency tests. The lecture's most important intellectual service is that it refuses to overstate what this body of work has achieved. Saying plainly that non-SUSY duality has no independent cross-validation beyond anomaly matching gives the later DSM construction a clearer, more honest footing.

## Concerns
The weakness is not lack of caveat but density of status changes. The lecture moves from exact SUSY tools, to a broken-SUSY toy model, to a hard obstruction, to anomaly-based conjectures, to large-`N` perturbative evidence, to recent applications. Because those categories are compressed into one narrative, a careful reader can still lose track of which sections are reporting exact structure, which are offering heuristic support, and which are simply laying out a working framework.

There is also a mild rhetorical pressure toward cumulative plausibility. Phrases like "remarkably coincides" or "compelling" are always qualified later, but the accumulation of examples can still feel like a rising evidentiary stack even though the lecture's own conclusion is that no new independent non-perturbative pillar has been added.

## Suggestions
- Add a short status tag at the start of each case study: "toy model," "obstruction," "anomaly-based candidate," or "large-N perturbative evidence."
- When moving from Cheng-Shadmi to Sannino's later programme, state once more that the later work sidesteps rather than resolves the broken-SUSY tension.
- In the recent-developments section, explicitly say that breadth of application is not the same thing as new independent validation.
