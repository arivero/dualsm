# Lecture 05 XHigh Assessment

## Source
`lectures/lecture-05.tex`

## Scope
This lecture introduces chiral gauge dynamics, the MAC criterion, the first tumbling step in the Georgi-Glashow `\SU{5}` example, Fradkin-Shenker complementarity, and the way those ideas motivate later non-SUSY duality claims. Its job is not to prove non-SUSY duality; it is to build the conceptual ladder from controlled ingredients to a later conjectural programme.

## Strengths
The lecture's main strength is its internal discipline. The MAC section does not merely mention caveats in passing: the warning box spells out four concrete limitations, including the absence of a theorem, the use of perturbative one-gluon exchange at strong coupling, known counterexamples when channels compete, and the fact that MAC identifies a channel rather than a scale. That framing matters, because the later `\SU{5}` calculation is then read correctly as a guided heuristic exercise rather than as a derivation of exact IR dynamics.

The `\SU{5}` example is also well chosen and well used. It walks the reader through channel enumeration, Casimir comparison, symmetry breaking `\SU{5} -> \SU{4}`, and then a deliberately unresolved anomaly-matching check. The `\UU{1}_B^3` mismatch is pedagogically effective precisely because the file explains that anomaly matching does not fail; instead, it shows that MAC alone does not determine the surviving massless spectrum. The complementarity section is similarly careful: it states the theorem clearly, gives the Higgs/confinement particle identification, and then restricts the theorem to the cases where it actually applies, including explicit counterpressure from adjoint Higgs, partial breaking, and the Shifman-Yung example. The later comparison table and preview box further prevent conceptual collapse by separating tumbling, complementarity, and duality as distinct claims with different evidence levels.

## Concerns
The lecture is strongest as a conceptual organizer, not as a technically closed tumbling analysis. Because it stops after the first `\SU{5} -> \SU{4}` step and defers the full massless-spectrum resolution, the worked example remains illustrative rather than fully executed. That is a reasonable pedagogical choice, but it means the reader never sees a complete tumbling cascade carried to an unambiguous endpoint inside this lecture itself.

The bridge to duality is also intentionally motivational, yet it sometimes sits close to the edge of sounding inferential. Even with the preview box and comparison table, the move from "tumbling plus complementarity gives a physical picture" to "this motivates a magnetic description" still depends on analogy and shared quantum numbers, not on a new non-perturbative result inside the lecture.

## Suggestions
- Keep the present warning-box structure, but add one sentence at the start of the duality-connection section stating that the bridge is motivational rather than deductive.
- If the notes are expanded later, the most valuable addition would be a brief appendix or follow-on example showing one more tumbling step or a more explicit account of the surviving `\SU{4}` spectrum.
- When the lecture invokes Pati-Salam as the cleaner setting for complementarity, a short reminder of why complete breaking matters there would make the forward link even tighter.
