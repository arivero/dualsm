# Lecture 08 XHigh Assessment

## Source
`lectures/lecture-08.tex`

## Scope
This lecture is the quantitative core of the dualised Standard Model story. It develops scalar democracy from the universal Yukawa structure of Lecture 7, introduces Planck-suppressed flavour operators, derives induced VEVs in the 18-Higgs sector, matches the duality scale to the electroweak and QCD scales, and closes with collider-facing consequences and an explicit "does/does not do" assessment.

## Strengths
The lecture's biggest strength is how aggressively it polices over-reading. The convention box frames the entire discussion as parametric consistency with `O(1)` coefficients, not as first-principles prediction. That discipline is then repeated where it matters most: the top-mass estimate, the `v ~ \Lambda_D^2 / M_{Pl}` relation, and the `\Lambda_{QCD}` running argument all come with warning boxes or remarks making clear what is input, what is free, and what is only an order-of-magnitude check. The closing "What the dualised SM does not do" section is especially strong because it collects the caveats into one place instead of hiding them in footnotes.

The lecture is also effective when it explains the conceptual shift. The universal Yukawa formulas cleanly show how flavour hierarchy is moved from the Yukawa matrices into the VEV pattern, and the one-light-plus-seventeen-heavy picture of electroweak symmetry breaking gives the reader a workable low-energy interpretation of a very crowded Higgs sector. The scale-matching section succeeds because it does not pretend to do more than it actually does: it gives a seesaw-style intuition for `\Lambda_D`, then uses one-loop running to show that the QCD scale lands in the right window rather than claiming precision.

## Concerns
The real weakness is density, not rhetorical excess. The hardest part of the lecture is the chain from Planck-suppressed four-fermion operators, to scalar mixing masses, to induced dormant-Higgs VEVs, to tiered fermion masses. Every step is stated, and the cited equations are clear in isolation, but the overall causal thread is easy to lose because the derivation moves quickly through several layers of matching and dimensional analysis.

There is also one important place where the source leans on an external dynamical assumption: `H_0` is the singled-out direction that gets negative mass-squared and triggers EWSB, but the lecture does not really derive why that direction wins beyond citing radiative corrections or strong dynamics. That is acceptable, but it means the mechanism is only partly internal to this lecture.

## Suggestions
- Add a one-page summary table listing inputs, induced quantities, and genuinely free parameters.
- Insert a short dependency diagram: Planck operators -> mixing masses -> induced VEVs -> effective Yukawas -> fermion masses.
- In the EWSB section, state more explicitly that the choice of the dominant Higgs direction is a model assumption or dynamical input, not a result established here.
