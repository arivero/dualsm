# Lecture 1 XHigh Assessment

Source: `lectures/lecture-01.tex`

Scope: Introduction and course framing; conventions; gauge-anomaly review; 't Hooft anomaly matching and spectator proof; the QCD warm-up; general `SU(N_c)` anomaly coefficients; the conformal-window discussion; and the closing summary/preview.

Strengths: The lecture has a strong pedagogical backbone. It cleanly separates gauge-anomaly cancellation from 't Hooft anomaly matching, and it reinforces that distinction with an explicit comparison table rather than leaving it implicit. The spectator-field proof is laid out in usable steps, so the exactness claim feels motivated rather than merely asserted. The source is also better signposted than a casual read might suggest: it already contains an `U(1)_A` warning box, an “Important caveats” list, a collected table of the six UV anomaly coefficients, a warning box on the model dependence of the non-SUSY conformal-window lower edge, an exact SUSY preview box, a phase table and figure, and a real summary bridge into Lecture 2. In other words, caveats, tables, and forward bridges are already present and doing real work. The conformal-window material is especially well handled at the level of epistemic hygiene: the upper asymptotic-freedom bound is treated as exact, the lower non-SUSY boundary is flagged as debated, and the SUSY result is clearly marked as a preview.

Concerns: The weakest passage is the QCD warm-up. The lecture’s own conventions section notes that `SU(2)` has no cubic anomaly, yet the warm-up computes a nonzero `SU(2)_L^3` anomaly by assigning `A(fund_SU(2)) = 1`, then uses that mismatch to motivate the WZW contribution. That is not just brisk exposition; it is an internal conceptual inconsistency. The WZW bridge itself is already present and reasonably clear, but it is attached to the wrong anomaly channel, which undercuts trust in the example. A second, smaller issue is the opening “course goal” list. Items about deriving the fermion hierarchy, the Fermi scale, and `Lambda_QCD` are much broader than what Lecture 1 itself establishes, so the framing reads more like a programme-level promise than a lecture-level deliverable.

Suggestions: Keep the overall structure. The main repair is to replace or rewrite the warm-up so the anomaly being matched is one that actually exists in the chosen example. If the intent is to teach how spontaneous symmetry breaking and the WZW term preserve anomaly information, the lecture should either switch to a flavour group with a genuine cubic anomaly or explain explicitly that the current `SU(2)` example is schematic and not a literal `SU(2)^3` computation. After that, trim or relabel the course-goal bullets so Lecture 1 promises tools and later payoffs rather than implying it already derives those phenomenological scales. With those changes, the lecture would remain well organized while removing its main source-level credibility problem.
